### B. Compare packaging strategies for this exact project limit

| Strategy | Exact definition | Verify / cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Complete-page topic bundles** | Concatenate **copies** of compatible PDFs into one or a few topical **derivative** PDF attachments, keeping full original printed pages, front provenance manifest, original ISBN/DOI/filename/SHA index and a page-offset map; preserve separate archived originals byte-for-byte. | **Pros:** max original content per slot. **Cons:** large files, duplicate pages, harder retrieval, new combined pagination and potential loss of PDF metadata/bookmarks/links/embedded files on some merge workflows. Verify every page count, rendered page/figure and searchable text, TOC and offsets. Does not materially reduce total PDF bytes. |
| **B. Evidence-only project composites** | Keep original PDFs fully archived and create targeted, **non-lossless editorial extracts** keyed per claim to paper/page/table/figure, species/scenario, units, evidence class (measured, guidance, inference, game-tuned), disagreement and applicability. | **Pros:** small, relevant, efficiently searchable project context. **Cons:** nuanced methods/tables may be omitted; a curated derivative is not an original. | **C. Hybrid (preferred subject to audit)** | Maintain complete original PDFs unchanged in Drive/local authorised archive, existing four composite research layers with small dated addenda for genuinely new research, and a **compact project research manifest** pointing to full originals. Retain a few selected complete originals in project slots only where source tables/figures/page-level review are central. | **Pros:** robust evidence + useful context, fewer active slots, no false “nothing lost” claim, easier maintenance. **Cons:** requires archive availability, manifest/source-link upkeep and periodic audit. |

Evidence extraction inevitably selects information and is not lossless; its coverage must be testable.
