**Interpretation of the apparent contradiction:** RSA's backing-in guidance is a sound **exit direction** principle for an ordinary driveway; HSA permits a **risk-assessed, controlled reverse when reversing cannot reasonably be eliminated**; Teagasc's ordinary forest haulage design expects **drive in, turn, exit**; Coillte asks hauliers to **minimise reversing** and retain turning space. Those statements are not interchangeable. An H1 lorry reverse-in is an **exceptional candidate to test** against road/transport/forestry duties, not the Irish default, and cannot be approved merely by observing that no on-site U-turn is needed.

**Distinct public-road rules:** the vehicle may need to perform part of its manoeuvre while on a public road; **the legality and safety of that movement** require site-specific consideration under Regulation 12 and traffic-management powers. **Public-road timber loading** is a separate issue that Teagasc explicitly rules out. All loading, crane reach, stacks, strapping and vehicle risk zone must fit **off** the road, on properly controlled land, or move to a separately approved site.

### Real forestry evidence separated from the model

* [Teagasc, **Silvicultural characteristics of oak and beech**, **16 June 2023**](https://teagasc.ie/news--events/daily/silvicultural-characteristics-of-oak-and-beech/): both native oaks are relatively **light-demanding**; sessile oak may survive some shade when young, but prolonged shade gives poor form/growth. European beech is shade-tolerant when young, **not** immune to severe shade; Teagasc cautions that under **25% daylight** biomass/branch development suffer. Sessile oak requires appropriately drained soil; beech prefers moist freely draining, sufficiently fertile soils. Do **not** equate the game's 0.221/0.0014 numbers directly with field percentage daylight.
* [Teagasc, **Natural Regeneration in Irish Woodlands: Seedling Establishment**, **26 April 2024**](https://teagasc.ie/news--events/daily/natural-regeneration-in-irish-woodlands-seedling-establishment/): tree recruitment involves light, competition from bramble/bracken/grass, moisture/soil and browsers. Oak can be suppressed under closed canopy; beech regeneration benefits at gap edges with intermediate openness, but more exposure can also release ground competitors. This article primarily discusses **natural regeneration** — species mechanisms transfer qualitatively to planted juveniles, not as proven planting-outcome rates.
* [Forest Research, **Sessile oak species database**](https://www.forestresearch.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/tree-species-database/131560-sessile-oak-sok/), British practice transfer: calls the species light-demanding, urges browsing protection and weeding, avoids wet/compacted sites, recommends appropriate group/nurse structure for timber form. Its British production densities **are not** prescriptions for eight saplings on this Irish 0.09ha game plot.
* [Forest Research, **Beech species database**](https://www.forestresearch.gov.uk/tools-and-resources/tree-species-database/beech-be/), British practice transfer: shade-tolerant juveniles may persist in understorey and respond to release, but establishment is slow, moisture/site matters and browsers can damage it. Shade tolerance ≠ positive growth at the source model's near-zero G2N/G2W light.
* [CCF pre-existing ecology/competitor research](https://github.com/jermur788/CCF/blob/main/Docs/Project/research-index.md) is supporting evidence, not authority to calibrate new coefficients or bypass the existing browse/understorey systems.

**Key biological distinction:** The source-marking method tests legal **spatial admission**, not a real root-zone survey, soil pH, drainage, crown branch clearance, response to actual H1 historical omissions, multi-year light, browse, shelter or stochastic survival. Adult Oak/Beech comparable modelling depth under D-059 does not make the G1/G2 light numbers scientific Irish physiological thresholds. A deliberately poorly lit planting can be educational as failure risk, but should not be described as an expected novice success.
