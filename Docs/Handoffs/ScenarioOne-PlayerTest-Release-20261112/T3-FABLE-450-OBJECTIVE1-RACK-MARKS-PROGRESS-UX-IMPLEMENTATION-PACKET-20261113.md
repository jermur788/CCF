## 1. Plain-English purpose and why

**Help the player find and spray-mark five rack entrance trees, without inventing five player-facing rack identities.** The yellow direction arrow initially suggests the **physical bottom rack nearest the access pad**, not whichever rack happens to have been created first. The HUD, minimap and under-reticle spray guidance should say **"rack mark"/"next rack mark"** without a rack number. Progress should read **"Rack marks: 0 of 5"**, then **1 of 5**, **2 of 5**, … **5 of 5**, depending on the number of distinct confirmed racks *successfully spray-marked*, **irrespective of the visit order**.

**Distinguish three different concepts**:
1. An authoritative `RackSaveData.rackId` remains a stable internal identity for a real physical rack and its work orders. **Never renumber it.**
2. A suggested *next target* is an unmarked physical entrance to show by compass arrow/3D cue/minimap, beginning south/bottom from the access pad. It is a navigational suggestion, **not a required progression order**.
3. A **mark count** is derived from persisted `entranceMarked` state of each rack and shows progress. Mark a different rack first? Still **1 of 5**. A second distinct rack? **2 of 5**. Re-spray an already marked rack? Still **2 of 5**.

The onboarding/lesson may explain once: *"Spray the entrance tree of each extraction rack to create a rack mark."* Subsequent UI may say **"Next rack mark"**, **"Rack marks: X of 5"**, **"Rack mark already made"**. Avoid **"Rack 1"**, **"Rack 2 entrance"**, or any `Rack N` identity in the guided **field** arrow/minimap/prompt. The **Objective 1 of N** curriculum label, **Racks planned X of 5** in the initial *map-planning* stage, tree IDs, and ordinary forestry terminology must not be conflated with this change.

**This newest direct user instruction supersedes** the earlier direction to name the south/bottom rack "Rack 1" and label the others 2–5. The physical initial suggested destination remains the south/bottom entrance, but the player sees no rank there.
