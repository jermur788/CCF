## Stage A — provisional independent five-forest portfolio (frozen before reading other proposals)

**Design rule:** each scenario should ask a different question visible at eye level when the player returns to the same forest decades later. They are separate starting forests, not five upgrades of one save; no money, equipment, stems or species are carried from one to another. Scenario One remains the accepted Irish reference, not a new redesign. The others are **proposals**.

| # | Working title and starting stand | One player-facing management question | First grounded action | Reason it is different |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Inherited Plantation** — a bounded, regular Irish Sitka stand, with some Oak/Beech in current game workflow | How can you draw income and access a mature crop while creating conditions for subsequent generations beneath maintained cover? | Walk one sample, choose crop trees and removals, plan rack access, commit only through Work Plan | Teaches the entire causal toolset and cost of commitments |
| 2 | **The Beech Shadow** — small mixed-age Oak/Beech farm woodland, some mature beech crowns suppressing oak advance growth | What do you keep when the most merchantable tree is also preventing the tree generation you want? | Inspect a shaded oak patch and retain nearby seed/crop/habitat trees; experiment with selective release and browser protection | The problem is the identity/location of retained trees, not plantation-wide removal percentages |
| 3 | **The Windward Ridge** — older, exposed Sitka-dominant hillside, awkward access and uneven edges | Will timber extraction now leave a stand stable enough to manage later? | Survey exposed edges and extraction approach before felling; choose conservative interior/lee-side treatments | Adds edge/exposure and disturbance risk, and the legitimacy of retaining an apparently overmature block |
| 4 | **The Oakwood Refuge** — small humid Atlantic native oakwood fragment, sparse saplings, browse lines, bracken and habitat trees | When does opening a canopy help oak recruitment, and when does disturbance damage the qualities you are trying to protect? | Walk an exclosure/monitoring transect; compare a low-disturbance path with targeted browse/competitor control | Ecological recovery, historical continuity, and defensible *no intervention* can matter more than revenue |
| 5 | **The Many-Aged Wood** — already structurally varied single bounded forest, with accessible and inaccessible patches, Oak/Beech/Sitka as an authored working mixture, and several recruitment cohorts | Can repeated small choices maintain useful timber, recruitment and habitat without slowly mining one age class? | Map current age/diameter/spatial structure and select a small first treatment *or hold* | Long-run feedback, decadal condition, uncertain forecasts, multiple acceptable steady/transition paths; not simply a larger map |

### Exploratory narrative beats and alternative endings, before feasibility check

1. **Inherited Plantation:** initial access/tree-selection work changes stand light; by a later visit growth partly closes the opening; by another, seedlings may be plentiful but recruitment to taller stages may remain poor. Options include slower transformation, a more open/planting-heavy trajectory with consequences, and a financially robust yet ecologically static result. A poor outcome is repeated extraction without secured regeneration or an insolvent plan. Learning: Work Plan commitment, crop-tree release, cost, light, browsing and repeated decisions.
2. **Beech Shadow:** a small removal at the first visit exposes suppressed oak; a later visit shows beech advance growth or bramble gaining instead; decades later an open oak patch may have lost continuity or a heavily retained patch may show no recruitment. Endings include oak-dominant renewal, mixed beech/oak structure with regeneration, or a low-income habitat-retention strategy; too much release or indiscriminate planting can also fail. Learning: species-specific competition/seed-source and protection.
3. **Windward Ridge:** a profitable thinning can make a new vulnerable edge; a retained strip may incur opportunity cost and shade; later storms could transform access, woody debris and value, **but only if a specifically accepted storm model is activated and qualified**. Endings include cautious staged thinning, cash-oriented harvesting with larger risk, or a restrained mature reserve. Learning: risk exposure and irreversibility, not a magic 'right windproof width.'
4. **Oakwood Refuge:** browsers can hold seedlings low despite abundant acorns; after protection, holly/other shade plants or bracken may still constrain oak; habitat continuity may benefit from restraint even when timber metrics do not. Endings include targeted regeneration successes, protected habitat with slow regeneration and a documented monitoring rationale, or costly disturbance without recovery. New species and habitat dynamics would need honest implementation and evidence.
5. **Many-Aged Wood:** first intervention can boost some crop trees; later entries may unintentionally reduce old/veteran cover or future seed/diameter supply; decades later different credible objectives yield different structures. Endings include fairly continuous income with renewing cohorts, conservation-led woodland with lower income, or an over-harvested canopy with a long recovery lag. Learning: sustainable *sequence* rather than one-time best mark.

### Alternative already generated without prior-plan consultation

A more ambitious fifth forest could replace **Many-Aged Wood** with **A Family's Patchwork Holding**, a connected player-level collection of several separately simulated woods and open habitats sharing one budget/crew calendar. This would make genuine property allocation and opportunity cost the endgame, but it is a high-risk new multi-stand state/UI/save and scheduling subsystem. At Stage A I tentatively prefer the single mature forest as the lean fifth: it keeps continuity of physical presence, isolates the core silvicultural challenge and reuses more of the existing bounded-forest engine.
