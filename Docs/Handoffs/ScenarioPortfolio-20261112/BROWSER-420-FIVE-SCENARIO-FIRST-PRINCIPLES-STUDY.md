**Design rule:** each scenario should ask a different question visible at eye level when the player returns to the same forest decades later. They are separate starting forests, not five upgrades of one save; no money, equipment, stems or species are carried from one to another. Scenario One remains the accepted Irish reference, not a new redesign. The others are **proposals**.

**Challenge to the Manager sketch:** the Manager's [separately opened sketch](MANAGER-FRESH-FIVE-SCENARIO-CONCEPT-SKETCH-20261112.md) converges strongly on the first four questions; that convergence is *not* evidence of independent blinding (see Stage A disclosure). I recommend **one deeply irregular, already many-aged working forest** as the first-five capstone, not an estate of several separately simulated woods. The estate is a strong *ambitious alternative* if a later architectural budget is accepted.

## Stage B — five complete scenario briefs and design trade-offs

The time frames below are **gameplay observation checkpoints**, not empirical predictions or evidence-backed fixed harvesting schedules. Positive/negative outcomes are intentionally conditional on future acceptance and verification of mechanics.

### 1. The Inherited Plantation — *teach a complete forest cycle*

- **Starting forest / visual identity:** retain the authored Irish, bounded even-aged Sitka plantation of Scenario One, headland entry/five parallel extraction racks, darker uniform canopy, small patches of Oak/Beech, existing human-scale paths and logs. **Do not change D-071/D-072 accepted access topology** in this proposal.
- **Central dilemma:** immediate timber and contractor/owner time versus preserving candidate crop/seed trees, canopy, access and a chance of taller regeneration.
- **Decisions:** choose and physically inspect the teaching sample; plan access on the approved Stand Map exception; individually mark retained crop vs fell trees; select disposition (sale/deadwood/retained timber), vegetation clearances, planted stock/shelters; review suggested wider treatment *without treating proposals as marks*; approve work; revisit reports and woods.
- **First action:** inspect an apparently similar pair of stems whose future functions differ. Learn that harvesting 10 trees from one patch is not equivalent to harvesting 10 elsewhere.
- **5 / 20 / 50 / 100-year narrative:** early sunlight, residue and economics; some canopy closure and visible establishment bottlenecks; later growing survivors and cohorts—or stalled height classes; eventually a more complex managed canopy or a tree-rich but non-recruiting and financially stressed stand. Individual outcome timelines depend on actually simulated state.
- **Learning / viable end states:** competence with the entire management loop, multi-intervention reasoning and transparency about why reproduction succeeded or failed. A careful income-led outcome, conservation-led treatment and slower mixed transformation may all be credible; repeated extraction with no replacement cohort or unsafe/unaffordable orders are legitimate failures. No rule that every successful player has to produce an 'optimal rainforest'.
- **Reuse/new debt:** highest reuse: ecology, tree marks, Work Plan/economy, browsing, bramble/bracken, planting, deadwood, racks, MFM and history. **Not yet an accepted complete external demo**; recent independent D-073 annual-review source check returned **NEEDS CHANGES** for observation-vs-delta provenance, and D-074/D-076 later-cycle extensions remain DRAFT. The successful #417 rendered gate is worker-reported and not acceptance of all Scenario One.

### 2. The Beech Shadow — *the right tree, not the right percentage*

- **Starting forest / visual identity:** compact mixed Oak/Beech farm wood: high domed beech crowns and copper leaf litter in shaded pockets; rougher, longer-lived oak stems, a local light well, some native border scrub represented honestly as decorative until given a causal ecological model. Oak can be sessile or pedunculate only where site provenance is supported; do not imply both native oak taxa are identical.
- **Dilemma:** beech is a viable timber tree but casts shade; leaving a large oak may preserve seed/habitat value yet suppress immediate commercial yield. Deer and competitive vegetation can foil both felling and planting.
- **Decisions / first action:** compare a veteran-quality oak and a vigorous beech as potential retained trees; inspect surrounding saplings and browse damage; decide whether to release a selected stem, make a small regeneration gap, maintain cover, shield a few vulnerable juveniles or wait.
- **5 / 20 / 50 / 100 years:** released crown/ground light (early); possible beech or bramble capture rather than oak recruitment; oak recruits either enter higher size classes or remain trapped; ultimately several plausible species proportions and timber/habitat structures. Avoid assuming a predetermined beech takeover from every initial condition.
- **Learning:** crown cast versus shade tolerance, seed source versus established saplings, local rather than bulk marking, quality versus habitat value, and what species-aware treatment really means. Good ends: productive mixed wood, intentional oak regeneration with cost, or retention-led mature wood. Failure: use Sitka-only prescription logic on broadleaves, irreversibly remove seed/habitat stems, or rely on seedlings that never recruit.
- **Reuse/new debt:** Oak/Beech parity code and existing mixed-occupancy **ordinal** design are promising; genuine native/introduced distinction, authored broadleaf trees and believable timber visuals needed. Existing Sitka-first MFM sample-to-comparable-area treatment must **not** be universally enabled by renaming a species. Need species-aware validity, asset/geometry authoring and qualification, a distinct broadleaf lesson, and a scenario identity/save separation. **Medium** because starting adults, juvenile browsing and work economics already exist, but no universal broadleaf calibration is proven.

### 3. The Windward Ridge — *stability has an opportunity cost*

- **Starting forest / visual identity:** sloping western-facing Sitka-dominant mature stand, lighter abrupt outer edge and windy exposed summit; sheltered lee interior contrasted with sparse wet hollows and a constrained extraction approach. Do not depict waterlogging as causal until site layers are supported.
- **Dilemma:** accessible merchantable stems tempt a strong intervention, yet new gaps, edges, height and soil/rooting conditions alter stability; retaining a strip can be costly and may keep the next regeneration patch too shaded.
- **Decisions / first action:** walk windward edges, compare route impacts, select whether a conservative interior treatment, staged edge retention, postponed operation or low-intervention zone is viable. If a storm system is separately enabled, consider safe salvage versus retained deadwood, replacement/understorey and contractor costs.
- **5 / 20 / 50 / 100 years:** changed stand geometry and income; edge/canopy recovery or storm-related damage **only with a qualified active disturbance model**; eventual reorganised patches or a vulnerable ageing structure; long-run species/structure choice changes exposure. No deterministic guarantee that a given gap causes a windthrow.
- **Learning/success/failure:** understand site-suitability triage: *CCF transformation is not right for every site*. Good outcomes include conservative profitability or habitat retention, not merely high timber volume. Failure includes unmanaged exposure loss or speculative risk arithmetic presented as forecast.
- **Reuse/new debt:** existing Rack V2/Work Plan, logs/deadwood, tree mortality, and **dormant** StormModel1 provide ingredients, not a fully featured storm scenario. A lean risk-only demonstration could use pre-authored exposure zones and intervention comparisons clearly labelled **gameplay scenarios**; a causal storm version needs versioned opt-in, incidence/intensity/effect provenance, site/topography interactions, safety salvage economy and full render/save checks. **Medium** as a scripted exposure judgement; **High** if storms become live.

### 4. The Oakwood Refuge — *sometimes the best cut is no cut*

- **Starting forest / visual identity:** narrow humid Atlantic oakwood on acidic slopes with moss/lichen-covered trunks, holly and rowan/birch edges where ecologically appropriate, scattered deadwood, small openings, exposed browsed leader shoots, bracken or bramble patches. The native-forest provenance and site template should be authored rather than generated by globally increasing an 'oak suitability' slider.
- **Dilemma:** protect historical habitat continuity and old tree values while encouraging replacement oak and associated woodland species. Removing browsing alone can allow dense holly/competition; making a canopy gap may harm locally valuable woodland microclimate. **'More saplings' is not a complete conservation objective.**
- **Decisions / first action:** compare browsed juveniles just inside/outside a protected sample and survey an old oak/deadwood/continuity feature. Choose monitoring/no work, targeted browsing intervention, limited vegetation control, careful edge/seed-source support or selective small-group release. Large-scale invasive removal is an **ambitious conditional addition**, not an existing action.
- **5 / 20 / 50 / 100 years:** early protection effects on leader damage; recruitment may remain blocked by shade/holly/ground flora; maturing recruits may preserve continuity if browsing/gaps are balanced; long-term woodland may stay habitat-rich with modest oak renewal, or degrade through chronic browsing/inappropriate disturbance. A modestly changing protected wood is **not automatically a failure**.
- **Learning/success/failure:** distinguish germination from successful recruitment, habitat age from forest continuity, and restraint from neglect. Accept conservation with monitoring, cautiously assisted recruitment and long-horizon habitat stability as viable outcomes; failure when unmonitored chronic browsing or invasive spread erases native regeneration/habitat.
- **Reuse/new debt:** core browsing + bramble/bracken juvenile competition, deadwood and visits/history can be adapted. **Major new debt** includes validated old-woodland goals/indicators, holly/hazel/rowan/birch as at least ordinal causal agents or honestly static visuals, geographic site fidelity, possibly rhododendron spread/containment, nature conservation economics and reversible/no-work review UX. An epiphyte/veteran score cannot be reverse-engineered from tree age alone. **High**; gate on expert ecology review and art-quality state mapping.

### 5. The Many-Aged Wood — *can a forest sustain its own management?*

- **Starting forest / visual identity:** one accessible but spatially heterogeneous bounded Irish mixed working forest, pre-authored with tall stems, middling crop trees, shaded/bright natural cohorts and a continuous uneven canopy. An old track/limited access exists; the challenge is **maintaining** a complex structure, not converting a new plantation.
- **Dilemma:** selectively harvest high-value large stems now while maintaining seed sources, next-crop diameter classes, saplings, canopy continuity and operating cash for later cycles. A forest can look prosperous for decades while drawing down the cohort needed to replace its largest trees.
- **Decisions / first action:** take a baseline walk across two differently structured patches, compare diameter/height/age and regeneration cohorts and mark either a modest release/harvest or an intentional holding year. Revisit the same patches through multiple truly distinct management cycles; question local outcomes and revise.
- **5 / 20 / 50 / 100 years:** visible retained/released crop crowns; growth of intermediate cohorts and losses from shade or browsing; changed cohort balance and deadwood; eventually multi-age continuity, age-class erosion, or a deliberately protected lower-revenue reserve. Future snapshots are diagnostics, not guaranteed equilibria.
- **Learning/success/failure:** uneven-aged selection is not 'always fell the biggest tree'. Measure replacement, repeatability, habitat and finances across several entries, compare alternative management narratives and their costs, and recognise that **a no-harvest decade** can be legitimate. Good: sustained recruitment plus harvest, slower low-extraction habitat path, or an adaptive transition that still protects future options. Failure: a high early revenue path leaving no next generation.
- **Reuse/new debt:** one forest world, tree/crown/regeneration, economics, year review/history, save/load and Work Plan. New work: authored multi-cohort inventory, meaningful cross-decade monitoring/diagnostics (size distribution, replacement trajectories and source provenance), scenario-specific completion and possibly revised access/management wording. Existing D-074/D-076 spaced/repeat management are **DRAFT**, so don't claim this capstone can run today. **Medium–High** for teaching/verification; far less architecture than simultaneously updating several stand worlds.

## Ecological and practitioner evidence: what it establishes (and does not)

1. **Irish transformation is not one thinning plus automatic natural regeneration.** Project research composite \`CCF_CCF_Practice_Economics_and_Transformation_Evidence_Composite.md\` §1 tracks the Irish LISS/TranSSFor/ContinuFOR four-thinning line and reports canopy closure/regeneration difficulties. The [Jones et al. 2026 study landing page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378112726006614), DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2026.124163, summarizes increased structural size diversity yet high basal area despite four interventions; note that the journal landing page displays an issue date **15 November 2026, three days after this report**, so treat the project’s earlier extracted source as research context, **not as a journal issue already published as of 12 November**. This supports Scenario One's repeated observation design, **not** a calibrated probability of regeneration success.
2. **CCF must be suitable to site.** [Teagasc CCF guidance](https://teagasc.ie/crops/forestry/advice/management/continuous-cover-forestry/) distinguishes simple-structure shelterwood and irregular selection, flags exposure/soil/wind stability and high browsing as conversion constraints; [Forest Research windthrow guidance](https://www.forestresearch.gov.uk/climate-change/risks/windthrow/) distinguishes exposure, drainage/rooting, tree height and thinning risk. This justifies Ridge choices and management uncertainty **qualitatively**; neither source supplies this game's Irish parcel windthrow coefficients.
3. **Species/site combinations matter.** [Teagasc Native Forest Framework](https://teagasc.ie/crops/forestry/grants/native-forest-framework/) distinguishes oak/birch/holly on podzols, oak/hazel on more fertile soils, alder/oak on gleys, pioneer birch on modified peat and alluvial forests. These are planting/framework types, **not instructions to turn all wet open habitat into woodland** or to simulate all species with Sitka density parameters. \`CCF Species Evidence Pack v1\` and \`CCF_Growth_Density_and_Species_Quantitative_Evidence_Composite.md\` classify non-Sitka Irish density evidence as scarce; mixed occupancy is architectural/ordinal unless validated per species.
4. **Deer exclusion creates complex trade-offs.** Perrin, Kelly & Mitchell's [2006 Killarney regeneration/stand study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037811270600911X), DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2006.09.025, and Perrin, Mitchell & Kelly's [2011 Killarney ground-flora study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112711005202), DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2011.08.028, concern long-running deer exclosures; the latter reports that blanket exclusion can promote holly/bramble and reduce certain specialist ground flora. This supports Oakwood Refuge **without** a universally optimal zero-deer rule, and it is not a basis for invented animal densities.
5. **Historical continuity is distinct from tree age.** [NPWS Irish woodland research](https://www.npws.ie/research-projects/woodlands) identifies ancient and long-established woods from historical evidence and cautions that individual indicator species do not prove antiquity. Scenario Four should author provenance/habitat goals instead of calculating 'ancient forest' from the age of planted trees.
6. **Irish invasive control is long-run work, not a click-to-cure.** The [Native Woodland Information Note on rhododendron](https://www.woodlandsofireland.com/wp-content/uploads/No.-3-The-Control-of-Rhododendron-in-Native-Woodlands.pdf) and [National Biodiversity Data Centre community account](https://biodiversityireland.ie/ivc-classification-explorer/in1/in1a/) describe suppression and need for follow-up against reinvasion. A future causal invasive mechanic needs lifecycle, spread/seed, repeated treatment and ecological validation, not a static instant-deletion action.
7. **Financial and work choices:** [Teagasc's current CCF support](https://teagasc.ie/crops/forestry/grants/woodland-improvement-scheme-2023-2027/wis-element-3-continuous-cover-forestry/) demonstrates staged management planning, protection and real cost categories; programme grants are not immutable game revenue. [Ireland's 2026 storm scheme](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-agriculture-food-and-the-marine/press-releases/reconstitution-scheme-for-windblown-forests-opens-for-applications/) confirms genuine owner-level storm consequences, not a calibrated storm probability.

**Evidence classes:** Irish empirical studies/DAFM/NPWS/Teagasc directly support the *types* of dilemmas; UK Forest Research is practice/transfer guidance; the stage/card outcomes, visual framing, hypothetical level layouts, time checkpoints and costs are **design inference**. Numeric treatment thresholds, individual-wind effects, growth forecasts and portfolio returns remain unsupported until separately calibrated/verified. The project's \`GROWFOR_Technical_Evidence_Extraction_v1.md\` supports Irish pure-stand growth model references but explicitly **not** CCF transfer validation.

## Stage C — practical lineup versus an ambitious alternative

The Manager's independent [nonbinding initial sketch](MANAGER-FRESH-FIVE-SCENARIO-CONCEPT-SKETCH-20261112.md) was opened **only after** Stage A was durably committed. It agrees on inherited plantation, broadleaf ambiguity, exposed forest and woodland recovery. Its separate multi-compartment **Living Estate** appears as the endgame. I regard that as a *strong late-stage long-term game vision*, but not the lowest-cost fifth forestry lesson. My Many-Aged Wood stresses genuinely irregular CCF and subtle cohort replacement before the game broadens to simultaneous holdings. This is a priority difference, not evidence that the Manager's concept is wrong.

### Two lineups explicitly compared

| Position | **Recommended lean five** (forestry-depth route) | **Ambitious five** (site/estate route) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inherited Plantation, existing Scenario One | Same existing Inherited Plantation |
| 2 | Beech Shadow, Oak/Beech judgement | **Wet-Edge Pioneer Forest**, native birch/alder/Scots pine mosaic and protected wet/open ground, site water/soil matching |
| 3 | Windward Ridge, initially cautious exposure teaching; active storms optional gated addition | **Storm-Broken Ridge**, site-aware disturbance, safe salvage, compound access/finance decisions |
| 4 | Oakwood Refuge, bounded browsing/habitat aims; new species/epiphytes/selected invasives only if separately supported | **Atlantic Woodland Recovery**, native minority species, invasive rhododendron, historic continuity and water/epiphyte interpretation |
| 5 | Many-Aged Wood, one richly structured forest and multi-cycle sustainability | **Patchwork Holding**, several independent forest compartments, shared money/labour/logistics and long-run estate choices |

The ambitious Wet-Edge world is an attractive more visually and ecologically distinct entry, but needs alder/birch/pine species models, vegetation/hydrology/open-habitat authoring and different silviculture and economics. Its risk of false precision is greater than Beech Shadow, which exploits existing parity and asks a difficult real question now. For the ambitious estate, *the player* carries knowledge between five independent scenarios, while within the final estate scenario several separately simulated substands share **one** new portfolio save. That is distinct from pretending five scenario saves form an estate.

| Evaluation | Lean five | Ambitious five | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning sequence | Clear: complete cycle → competing trees → risk → restraint → sustained irregular selection. Scenario Two teaches different *judgement*, not same tool tutorial | More varied ecology/portfolio, but flood of new variables can hide causal lessons | **Lean wins first-five pedagogy** |
| Visual/world variety | Good: Sitka rows, broadleaf shade, windward skyline, Atlantic oakwood, multi-layer forest; some overlapping woodland palette | Excellent: wet/open mosaic, storm damage, native rainforest recovery, cross-property landscape | **Ambitious wins spectacle/biome diversity** |
| Ecological credibility | Strong if scripted site assumptions declared and unsupported restoration scores avoided | High potential but high risk of unsupported species/water/disturbance/epiphyte numerical claims | **Lean wins reliability near-term; ambitious could win after field/research calibration** |
| Development effort / reuse | New authored worlds and lessons, but mostly one world/one yearly loop; biggest debt is restoration/risk | High-to-very-high species, site, storm, invasives and persistent multiple-world operations | **Lean wins reuse and safe sequencing** |
| Replayability/strategy | High if multiple legitimate end states, actual delayed cohorts and choices after years; no rote fixed answers | Very high if estate budgets/logistics and varied site types work | **Ambitious wins potential breadth, not presently proven playability** |
| Small-owner realism | Direct individual woodland owner choices and changing objectives | Multi-block family farms also credible, but could overemphasise industrial-style portfolio optimisation | **Lean closer to earliest target user; ambitious later** |

**Scenario Five choice:** A multi-woodlot estate is more varied but requires shared-budget scheduling, per-forest saves/history, maps, access and cross-world tests. A single irregular forest better exposes whether the existing forestry model can sustain repeat recruitment and timber supply. **Recommend Many-Aged Wood first, estate as later expansion.**
