## 1. Executive verdict

**Recommend progression B — Forestry craft and CCF depth first:**  
**1 Inherited Sitka Plantation → 2 Working Mixed Forest → 3 Neglected Broadleaf Wood → 4 New Farm Woodland → 5 Exposed Upland Forest → 6 Wet Woodland and River Valley → 7 Atlantic Oakwood → 8 Complete Irish Forest Estate.**

**Runner-up A — Beginner-to-competent-owner:** 1 → New Farm Woodland → Neglected Broadleaf Wood → Wet Woodland and River Valley → Working Mixed Forest → Atlantic Oakwood → Exposed Upland Forest → Complete Irish Forest Estate. A is more intuitive for novice farm-forest owners but has an immediate new-world establishment implementation cliff.

**C — Ecological/site diversity first** is a valid *different educational philosophy*, especially if conservation-led learners are the principal audience, but it fronts hydrology and long-continuity/invasive dependencies and risks teaching "ecological" outputs via unsupported proxy scores.

B is **not a licence to build mature irregular CCF by just applying Sitka yield curves**. Scenario Two is recommended early only if there is an evidence-honest **minimum three-species, spatially mixed, uneven-size/age proof**, a bounded new-scenario identity/save contract and reviewer-visible diagnostic limits. If that proof cannot be made without a speculative generic engine or misleading forecasts, switch to runner-up A **only after the establishment blocker is estimated**, or choose a constrained guided S2 bridge, not fake mature-CCF simulation.

**Non-negotiable:** the first external demo is the existing *complete* Scenario One with Sitka/Oak/Beech parity and its own D-064/D-071/D-072 Rack V2 five parallel headland-entry racks, physical entrance marks, D-065 MFM sample-to-comparable/work-plan gate, S1-C/D repeated management and old-save/Reference protections. #428 does not qualify, release or delay it. No Windows build before user personal playtest and explicit authorisation. Scenario 8's internal woodlots may share *that estate's* resources; the eight separate scenario saves never share trees, money or inventories.

## 2. Three materially different full progressions

### A. Beginner-to-competent-owner — scaffold the observation before complex harvesting

**Teaching hypothesis:** begin with obvious land and early-life signals, then teach health/soil, then advanced uneven-aged silviculture, and finally complex conservation/risk/estate choices. This makes the player first a careful assessor and cultivator, then an increasingly independent manager.

| Position | Case/title and one newly unlocked skill | True minimum scenario prerequisite, gate and staged overlap |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **O1 Inherited Sitka Plantation:** full coached sample → Work Plan → annual change → repeated intervention | #424 complete Scenario One demo qualification only. Do not alter it. |
| 2 | **O4 New Farm Woodland:** decide **whether to plant at all**; identify habitats/soil and protection | **BLOCKER A2:** scenario selection/saves + pre-forest habitat/establishment state and finance; no generic land simulation. During O1, research site maps/stock; any common save/edit must wait for serial owner. |
| 3 | **O3 Neglected Broadleaf Wood:** differentiate retained habitat trees from quality crop trees and ash disease | **BLOCKER A3:** authored health/disease state + specialist decisions; Oak/Beech core reuse. In parallel with O4: disease source audit, authored layout, paper spec; not shared ecology/save edits. |
| 4 | **O5 Wet Woodland and River Valley:** site-driven access/water consequences | **BLOCKER A4:** wet-site layers + causal disturbance/access indicator; alder/birch/willow representation at agreed depth. In parallel with O3: soil/river evidence and art feasibility; shared ground-state code serial. |
| 5 | **O2 Working Mixed Forest:** sustain income and multiple recruitment cohorts with less tutoring | **BLOCKER A5:** credible multi-age initial state and stand/size diagnostics, mixed competition provenance; avoid numerical CCF predictions. Prepare age-structure sensitivity independently of O5 wet-site implementation. |
| 6 | **O6 Atlantic Oakwood:** protect history, not just plants; compare deer/holly/invasives | **BLOCKER A6:** causal repeated rhododendron state + native woodland continuity policy + recruitment visibility; research while O2 is qualified, integrate after own gate. |

Portfolio concepts may start early; shared persistence only after prior baselines are stable. |

**Seven explicit learning transitions:**
1→2: the player takes established-stand inspection/planting lessons from O1 to *land without a forest* and must first justify creation.  
2→3: compare a planned new crop with a neglected older crop; age, form and disease change treatment.  
3→4: transfer site/stock diagnosis from broadleaf to a place where **water limits where work is possible**.  
4→5: transfer local heterogeneity to irregular, already productive CCF; now balance several age strata and income.  
5→6: use careful retention/recruitment skills but change the objective from long-run timber flow to historical native habitat.  
6→7: transfer deliberate restraint, site sensitivity and retention to a dramatic exogenous hazard where immediate salvage is not automatically best.  
7→8: integrate competing constraints without a single compulsory prescription; compare unmet needs and budget priorities.

**Pros (concrete):**
- **P1 Learning:** new owners can recognise soil suitability, protective planting and failure to establish before interpreting subtle uneven-aged volume changes.
- **P2 Authenticity:** makes *no-plant/no-work* visible early rather than treating all land as vacant plantation space.
- **P3 Audience fit:** farm-forest owners often face establishment and protection as tangible management tasks.
- **P4 Fading help:** starts with a familiar O1 workflow, then a visually distinctive open-site decision, with O2 advanced mixed stand delayed until judgement grows.
- **P5 Ecological differentiation:** wet soils and site risk become explicit prior knowledge for later mixed-stand diagnosis.

**Cons (concrete):**
- **C1 High early engineering debt:** O4 needs a genuine pre-forest habitat/ground and species-establishment state, new save/scene identity and cost path immediately after O1.
- **C2 A novice may be overloaded** by site maps, planting policy and habitat exclusions just after a long, complete O1 curriculum.
- **C3 Early O3 ash dieback** adds hazardous health/reconstitution decisions before the player has extensive broadleaf silvicultural practice.
- **C4 O5 before O2** postpones the major CCF payoff promised by the reference game; CCF depth feels interrupted by more land-site tasks.
- **C5 Reuse disadvantage:** several new habitat/water features start before repeated harvesting/marking mechanics have been exercised in another forest.

**Worked journey:** In O1 the learner saved a crop tree and observed recruitment after opening light. In O4 they find a field edge with regenerating scrub and a valuable unplanted wet hollow; they keep the hollow open, plant only a suitable strip, and discover failed unprotected juveniles by Year 5. In O3 they ask not “how many should I plant?” but “which mature trees are worth releasing, and which risky ash need professional review?” This transfers **assessment before action**, but not a universal planting/thinning rule.

**Overlap/serial judgement:** Research O3 disease and O5 site ecology during O4 implementation **yes**, separate documents/asset mockups. New shared species/save/juvenile/cell models **no** until the active writer releases the relevant files. Each completed scenario gets exactly one qualification candidate and single Unity lease; no parallel S2/S3 Unity.

### B. Forestry craft and continuous-cover depth first — **RECOMMENDED**

**Teaching hypothesis:** O1 is a substantial reference tutorial, not a trivial level. Reinforce its new forestry judgement in a different already-managed stand *before* adding completely new site and hazard systems. Move from crop-tree and local silviculture to establishment, disturbance, water, conservation, finally portfolio decisions. Existing player interaction has leverage rather than being replaced with many new control schemes.

|---|---|---|

| 2 | **O2 Working Mixed Forest:** maintain *already irregular* mixed canopy, protecting multiple future cohorts | **BLOCKER B2:** bounded scenario identity/new-game/objectives/save, authored multi-age/size state and mixed light/growth/recruitment diagnostics. **Do not generalise a new engine first.** O3 broadleaf studies/spec draft can proceed separately while O2 is built. |
| 3 | **O3 Neglected Broadleaf Wood:** judge broadleaf quality/habitat plus ash health and safe referral | **BLOCKER B3:** causal/recorded ash condition (could be authored stages, not epidemiological spread), professional work constraints and reconstitution choices; Oak/Beech marking reuse. O4 land suitability evidence can overlap. |
| 4 | **O4 New Farm Woodland:** establish suitable woodland and sometimes decline planting | **BLOCKER B4:** pre-forest habitat/land, site suitability, establishment survival and independent forestry budget; study/stage landscape while O3 completes. |
| 5 | **O7 Exposed Upland Forest:** manage operational risk, wind, storm and recovery | **BLOCKER B5:** verify/activate dormant StormModel1 in a constrained new forest, site/exposure, safe contractor-only salvage and recovery; exposure/soil research can overlap O4; StormModel changes serial. |

| 7 | **O6 Atlantic Oakwood:** native continuity, browse/holly and repeat invasive work over decades | **BLOCKER B7:** native habitat continuity, causal rhododendron/reinvasion and recruitment; long-term habitat art is not proof of modeled state. Advance research independently during O5. |
| 8 | **O8 Complete Irish Forest Estate:** reconcile timber, water, risk, restoration and budgets over several compartments | **BLOCKER B8:** single-estate compartment identity, portfolio finance/annual work, historical lineage/QA. Design ledger early; integrate only once the other systems have bounded interfaces. |

**Seven explicit learning transitions:**
1→2: O1 teaches **transforming** a uniform stand; O2 asks how to **maintain** an already uneven-aged productive stand without harvesting the next generation. Same work loop, different starting structure and target.  
2→3: O2's multi-species competition becomes finer-grained crop quality, disease and habitat-tree judgements in mature broadleaves rather than generic “keep structure”.  
3→4: from rehabilitation of old trees, switch to establishment decisions when *no crop yet exists*; practice site-first species choices and cost/protection.  
4→5: previously chosen sites/young stock now meet exposure, soil stability and a damaged mature stand; recognise hazard versus routine treatment.  
5→6: extend the exposure/rooting/soil operational assessment to wet valley water and access consequences, without turning a river into an annual flood dice-roll.  
6→7: retain wet-site restraint and sapling-recruitment measurement but add **historical continuity** and repeated invasives that cannot be restored by rapid planting.  
7→8: choose and justify priorities across commercial and conservation compartments with limited common resources.

**Pros (concrete):**
- **P1 Engineering leverage:** O2 can initially use accepted Sitka/Oak/Beech plus existing marking, Work Plan, browse, history, economy and visible feedback; the first new heavy system is *scenario identity*, not hydrology/invasives.
- **P2 Pedagogical continuity:** immediate contrast between transforming and maintaining a CCF stand tests whether O1 understanding transfers to an independent forest.
- **P3 Evidence honesty:** existing individual-tree data can show local competition and recruitment explicitly, while long-horizon irregular growth remains labelled modelling inference/uncertainty rather than falsely calibrated yield.
- **P4 Production milestones:** can qualify a genuine second complete forest before requesting wide species roster or property-generalization work.
- **P5 Visible decisions:** releasing a crop tree, retaining a seed tree, or delaying harvest alters subsequent structure on walks without requiring spectacular unmodelled events.
- **P6 Scheduling:** broadleaf disease evidence, establishment research and separate art can prepare while O2 owns common code/Unity.

**Cons (concrete):**
- **C1 Novice jump:** an already irregular stand may be cognitively harder than O1's clear row and rack cues. Needs an honest short *orientation* on cohorts, not replaying the whole tutorial.
- **C2 Prediction credibility:** existing calibration derives heavily from even-aged Sitka and ordinal broadleaf/mixed occupancy; numerical claims about sustainable uneven-aged yields would exceed evidence.
- **C3 Lesson duplication risk:** O2 and O3 can become the same “pick crop trees under beech” puzzle unless O2 emphasises *cohort continuity + periodic yield* and O3 emphasises *quality + disease + retained habitat*.
- **C4 Blocker B3:** disease-specific ash status and specialist reconstitution are novel and can make O3 more expensive than its Oak/Beech reuse suggests.
- **C5 Momentum risk:** the opening two chapters both involve selective harvest; a genuinely changed forest structure, objective and review frame must make O2 visibly different.
- **C6 Risk of spectacle:** O7's storm must be causally simulated, saved and qualified; decorative windblow would undermine evidence and education.

**Worked journey:** O1 player selects a retained Sitka crop tree, opens surrounding light and sees delayed Oak/Beech growth. In O2 a valuable mature oak is a seed source above a scarce younger cohort. They compare harvesting it for cash now, taking a neighbour instead, or waiting a review cycle; young height classes and income change. In O3 the same choice is no longer enough: a good-form oak may need targeted competitor release, but dying ash needs an independently recorded risk/referral/reconstitution decision and an old hollow stem may be retained for habitat. The learner uses prior crown/seed reasoning but must acquire **tree quality and disease triage**.

**Overlap/serial judgement:** While O2's *one* implementation lane owns new-scenario identity and species/forest ecology, O3 may undertake ash guidance review, authored site observations, acceptance-criterion drafts and read-only static audits; **no O3 programmer edits shared save, ecology, Work Plan, scene or tests yet**. After O2 handoff/qualification/integration, O3 can become the production owner. Similarly, O4 data/spec preparation during O3; O7 wind site/risk research during O4; O5 hydrology during O7; O6 continuity/invasion during O5; O8 paper portfolio design throughout without shared architecture edits. All requires real claims, disjoint file sets and #424/local machine lease discipline.

### C. Ecological and site diversity first — conservation-first, widest early contrast

**Teaching hypothesis:** build site/habitat literacy and teach restraint before progressing to production and disease-intensive forest management. The eight become a series of different **ecological contexts**, with CCF craft returning as a late advanced application.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **O1 Inherited Sitka Plantation:** baseline work/review under existing acceptance boundary | #424 unchanged. |
| 2 | **O5 Wet Woodland and River Valley:** use moisture gradient to limit action and retain water/soil | **BLOCKER C2:** hydrology-light but causal wet-site/access layers; alder/birch/willow site traits. O6 flora/invasives research can overlap without code. |
| 3 | **O6 Atlantic Oakwood:** weigh habitat continuity, deer and rhododendron against active work | **BLOCKER C3:** actual invasive state, repeated treatment, recruitment/browse differentiation, authored legacy and provenance. O4 afforestation feasibility research can overlap. |
| 4 | **O4 New Farm Woodland:** choose whether and where to establish a new forest *after* learning habitat value | **BLOCKER C4:** open land/habitat/non-plant options, species suitability, new juvenile economy. O7 risk/site audit separate. |
| 5 | **O7 Exposed Upland Forest:** safety/soil exposure with windblow and recovery | **BLOCKER C5:** activated and verified storm/salvage/contractor workflow, site risk and old-save policy. O3 disease research can overlap. |
| 6 | **O3 Neglected Broadleaf Wood:** recover quality and ecology while dealing with ash health | **BLOCKER C6:** credible ash state/reconstitution/professional oversight and broadleaf inventory. O2 irregular-growth feasibility can overlap. |
| 7 | **O2 Working Mixed Forest:** late synthesis of productive multi-age continuous-cover judgement | **BLOCKER C7:** honest irregular mixed-stand ecology, timber flow/recruitment diagnostics and long-horizon calibration caveats. Capstone paper model can overlap. |
| 8 | **O8 Complete Irish Forest Estate:** balance mixed silviculture and ecology with estate finance | **BLOCKER C8:** compartment budget/schedule/history/independent world saves; serial integrated QA. |

**Seven explicit learning transitions:**
1→2: O1 introduces woodland outcomes; O5 immediately challenges its apparent applicability where saturated soil and water/terrain outrank extraction.  
2→3: shift from hydrological site limits to continuity of a previously existing native community; not all protection goals are water goals.  
3→4: ask whether a *new* woodland is beneficial after seeing why old woodland continuity cannot be bought by planting.  
4→5: apply species/site suitability and owner liability to an existing exposed production crop and storm recovery.  
5→6: connect risk/referral to diseased broadleaves, then re-introduce crop-tree quality and value.  
6→7: translate broadleaf quality judgement to whole-stand multi-age production where successive cohorts need long-term balance.  
7→8: apply the now complete ecology and silviculture toolset across a common property budget.

**Pros (concrete):**
- **P1 Ecological integrity:** forces early attention to soil, wetland and historic woodland exceptions rather than treating every forest as a timber factory.
- **P2 High visual differentiation:** stream edge, old oakwood and open farmland contrast immediately with straight Sitka plantation rows.
- **P3 Owner risk literacy:** early recognition that no-work and specialist habitat advice can be responsible management choices.
- **P4 Strong pre-planting context:** seeing mature native woodland *before* new planting discourages instant “plant enough = make ancient woodland” thinking.
- **P5 Conservation audience fit:** aligns with landholders whose goals are nature, water and woodland protection.

**Cons (concrete):**
- **C1 Very high early engineering debt:** wet soil/access, species roster, invasives and legacy ecology all arrive before a second modest CCF implementation is proven.
- **C2 False-science risk:** invented water-quality numbers, epiphyte/biodiversity scores or rhododendron reinvasion rates would be easy shortcuts and hard to justify.
- **C3 Poor immediate reuse:** a broad species/habitat engine may displace the current tested crop-tree/Work Plan gameplay before it is proven across forests.
- **C4 CCF momentum fades:** learners attracted by the working-forest premise may not practise commercial selective harvest again until O3/O2 late in the sequence.
- **C5 High QA/serial risk:** multiple new causal systems and saved state would require independent checks before each playable gate; overlap of research does not overlap Unity.
- **C6 Dangerous framing:** if "leave it alone" is the default answer everywhere, the important active management of invasives, browse and unsafe windblow could be obscured.

**Worked journey:** O1 player learns to release a young oak. In O5 they discover a wetter hollow where alder or willow is better suited and ground traffic damages soil. In O6 they find oak seedlings abundant but recruitment missing, rhododendron returning and old canopy/habitat irreplaceable on a short planting timeline. Their choice is to monitor sapling classes and control invasive/browsing pressure with low-disturbance operations rather than automatically clear a gap. O4 then asks *where should a forest begin*, not *how fast can we plant it*.

**Overlap/serial judgement:** O6 ecological literature and asset references can be prepared during O5; O4 soils/planting standards during O6; O7 exposure/storm evidence during O4; O3 disease during O7; O2 mixed-stand analysis during O3; O8 finance constraints during O2. **Not** parallel: wet-ground and native-regeneration source writes on same ecology files; simultaneous Unity Editor/serialization; shared schema migrations or scene/prefab edits before single-writer handoff.

## 3. Eight-by-skill curriculum and case differentiation

This is **per original case**, usable in any ordering; the learner must complete the common six-part reflection **diagnose → compare/choose → authorise/hold → annual observation → justify/revise**. “Approve” is Work Plan commitment where work is eligible, not a permit or professional legal judgement. Each row describes **new** competence, not recurring tutorial screens.

| Case | Diagnose (new sign) | Choose (new judgement) | Approve / hold (new resource or boundary) | Observe (actual causal state required) | Justify / revise (learning evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 Sitka | density, crop tree, access, light, browse | competing tree, rack/sample, protect/retain | existing Work Plan and ownership/cost | recorded removals, light, recruitment, annual money over cycles | why candidate/more light is not an instant regenerating forest |
| O2 Working Mixed | age/diameter layers and successor cohorts | harvest a mature seed tree vs neighbour vs wait | selective timber and repeated treatment limits | retained growing stock, stage-class recruitment, successive harvest | sustainability is not just one positive annual income |
| O3 Neglected Broadleaf | oak/beech form, habitat/veteran, diseased ash condition | quality release, protect veteran, specialist ash route | competent contractor/reconstitution vs defer and monitor | release, form/retained stems, diagnosed ash-stage trajectory (if modelled) | quality improvement is not indiscriminate removal or automatic disease cure |
| O4 New Farm | soil, land-cover/habitat, scrub/hedge, plantable patches | where *not* to plant; seed vs stock/protection | establishment cost/stock/permission assumption and maintenance | planted survival and actual juvenile height classes vs non-plant | ecological benefit is conditional on site and existing habitat |
| O5 Wet Valley | moist/gley/alluvial gradient and access damage risk | keep buffer, light hand work vs extraction, species match | constrained timing/equipment; no-work wet area | persisted soil disturbance / accessible condition and regeneration | wetness is not a simple low fertility value; do not claim calculated flood reduction |
| O6 Atlantic Oakwood | veteran/continuity, oak seedling→sapling stall, rhodo/browse | repeat invasive work, browse management, restrained light | protected-area/ecology advice, monitor cycles, limited works | actual invasive regrowth and sapling recruitment plus old-tree retention | historical continuity ≠ stand age or newly planted canopy |
| O7 Exposed Upland | exposure/rooting/stability, windblow, hazard | avoid destabilising thin, safe isolate, salvage selectively/hold | competent contractor and permission for hazardous salvage | storm deaths/fallen states, money/access/recovery and species establishment | one profitable clearout can worsen future stability/site recovery |
| O8 Complete Estate | unequal stand urgency across compartments | schedule competing interventions and justified delay | one estate-local budget/labour/contractor allocation | per-compartment annual history plus aggregate resources | explain trade-offs, uncertainty and changed priorities after outcomes |

**Exact separation of O2/O3/O6:**  
- **O2 Working Mixed** = **production continuity problem**: already uneven age/diameter, retain a functioning successor cohort while generating periodic timber income. Relevant indicators: stand structure, recruiting size classes, spatial balance and harvested product. A healthy canopy tree may still be a poor harvesting choice. No invasive or diseased-ash requirement for the *core* proof.  
- **O3 Neglected Broadleaf** = **quality, rehabilitation and health problem**: formerly neglected even/mixed broadleaf patches, crop-tree form and direct competition, veteran value, **separately recorded ash dieback condition**, safety/referral/reconstitution. A poorly formed tree might become an appropriate competitor removal; a diseased ash is not just a less valuable timber item. A purely crop-tree selection lesson without ash triage risks duplication with O2.  
- **O6 Atlantic Oakwood** = **native continuity and regeneration recovery problem**: historical woodland structure, browsing/holly/light, rhododendron reinvasion, deadwood/veterans and low-disturbance strategy. A perfect income or timber-form outcome might be an ecological failure. **Not** a native-coloured variant of O3.  
- **O4 vs Stage 2:** It is *forestry establishment only*, with limited site/stock/maintenance/finances. It does not build orchard management, general farming, ponds, fencing construction, food production or property-land-use simulation.  
- **O5 vs O6:** O5's causal constraints are *wetness, access, water/soil protection, spatial site matching*; O6's are *past continuity, old substrates, browse/holly and invasive recovery*. A wet-looking oakwood or lichen model is not either system.

**Tutorial taper:** O1 full guided, O2/O4 (whichever comes second) partial walkthrough of one new state, positions 3–5 diagnostic prompts but no compelled choice, 6–7 optional field notes and decision comparison, O8 self-authored ten-year plan with transparent consequences. No XP ladder; no single exact tree marking answer. A learner may reasonably choose **monitor/no work** and demonstrate better management than a compelled thinning.

The player can hold, record why, then compare. At least one real work-and-review cycle plus a repeat observation/decision is required unless no-work is the defensible selected branch.
