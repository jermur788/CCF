# Independent eight-scenario Irish forestry learning portfolio — concept, 12 November 2026

**Status:** EXPLORATORY PRODUCT PROPOSAL / NOT ACCEPTED. **Author:** CCF Overall Manager. **Isolation:** separate proposal only; no change to decisions, milestones, specifications, production code, task ownership or the independent five-scenario study. No Unity verification or new playable scenario is claimed.

## Intent and boundaries

Retain Scenario One as the existing Irish even-aged Sitka-spruce-to-continuous-cover reference. Propose seven distinct complementary forest situations, so a player finishes able to *assess an unfamiliar Irish forest, identify plausible management choices, observe the consequences, and know when professional advice is needed*. This is **not** a professional qualification and cannot ensure competence for every forestry situation. Each forest is independent, with separate ownership, ecology, finance and history; only player learning transfers. The game loop remains inspect → decide/mark → Work Plan → simulated execution → annual ecology/economy → walk/review → revise.

## Proposed portfolio

| # | Scenario and starting condition | Main learner question | Distinct experience/decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Inherited Sitka plantation** — even-aged productive spruce, with Oak/Beech introduction | How can a commercial stand transition to repeated continuous-cover management? | Existing Scenario One: map-planned five parallel extraction racks, physical entry-tree marks, crop/fell choice, sample-to-comparable treatment, Work Plan, retention, regeneration/browsing and repeated Annual Review |
| **2** | **Working mixed forest** — productive multi-aged, multi-species CCF already established | How do you *maintain* an irregular forest without degrading its next generation? | Identify which cohorts/patches need release, retention or a longer rest; harvest selectively across successive cycles; distinguish immediate yield from future growing stock |
| **3** | **Neglected broadleaf wood** — oak/beech and diseased ash blocks, poor form and uncertain regeneration | How do you rehabilitate a crowded, damaged broadleaf wood without treating all trees alike? | Release promising crop trees, manage shade, keep veteran/habitat stems, assess ash dieback separately, consult competent professionals for unsafe trees; avoid normal ash crop replanting |
| **4** | **New farm woodland** — mixed mineral soils, existing hedgerows/scrub, some land unsuitable for planting | Where should woodland be established, which species fit, and is planting always justified? | Read soil/exposure/habitat, retain existing natural regeneration, choose groups/stock/protection, protect non-forest habitats, revisit establishment over decades and assess owner/contractor costs |
| **5** | **Wet woodland and river valley** — gleyed woodland, floodplain and stream buffer with dry microsites | How should management change when water and soil sensitivity dominate? | Site-driven alder/birch/willow/oak decisions, avoid unsupported drainage, protect water/soil during access, select limited interventions or deliberate non-intervention |
| **6** | **Atlantic oakwood** — long-established native woodland, rhododendron pressure, deer-browsed regeneration and legacy structures | How can a woodland recover while maintaining the continuity that makes it valuable? | Repeated invasive control, browsing exclosures/protection, oak-sapling recruitment rather than seedling-count success, veteran/deadwood retention, preserve humid understory |
| **7** | **Exposed upland forest** — commercial conifer stand with variable peat/mineral soils, windthrow exposure and a major storm | When is harvesting too risky, and how should a damaged plantation recover? | Wind/rooting/exposure, contractor-only hazardous salvage, market and access constraints, habitat/soil retention, choose reforestation and resilience without automatically replanting unsuitable bog |
| **8** | **Complete forest estate** — several independent forest compartments under one owner's budget | What should be done first, what can wait, and how do you balance all obligations? | Independent assessment, multi-year work programme, cash/labour limits, mature CCF timber, diseased/damaged patches, water/native habitat, uncertain events and final self-justified review |

## Signature scenario structure and assessment

1. **Scenario One: full guided reference.** Preserve the accepted layout/workflow and existing three-species depth. Require several interventions, genuine review and a player explanation of why changing timber take, gap placement or protection changed outcomes. **Do not claim a fully qualified external demo exists** until actual integration and player-test gates pass.
2. **Working mixed forest: maintain, do not convert.** Several age/size classes already coexist; lower storey is unevenly established. Some trees have high immediate timber value but are seed sources or essential to future structure. Distinct optimal actions depend on neighbourhood, light and regeneration. Assessment: sustain multiple recruitment stages, acceptable harvest/income and maintained cover over repeated cycles; no universal perfect marking pattern.
3. **Neglected broadleaf: quality and health.** Suppressed oak beside dominant beech, crowded quality stems, veterans, ash-dieback risk. Allow local crown release and species-aware thinning, but not indiscriminate clearance or treating diseased ash as a generic yield penalty. Assessment: credible regeneration and timber/retained habitat, traceable choices, professional safety/reconstitution decision for diseased ash.
4. **New farm woodland: site before species.** Start with a habitat and soils map, then walk it. Include a valuable existing open habitat as a genuine *do-not-plant* option; different mineral and wet microsites call for different assemblies. Revisit weed competition and browsing 1, 5, 15, 30 years later. Assessment: suitable living mixed woodland, efficient maintenance, habitats protected and honest costs.
5. **Wet woodland: water is a hard constraint.** Show a gradient between stream, floodplain/wet gley and drier bank. Timed wet-ground access or unnecessary drainage can cause failure; alder/birch/willow may outperform conifer/oak on unsuitable wet cells. Assessment: stable river edge, low damage, site-appropriate recruits, measured benefits rather than timber-only score.
6. **Atlantic oakwood: regeneration vs continuity.** Removing deer does not automatically grow oak: holly/ground competition, available light and browsing interact; invasives can return if not monitored. Make veterans, deadwood, woodland history and sensitive epiphyte habitat distinct from quick planted-tree counts. Assessment: sustained regeneration into sapling classes, invasive containment, protected old structures.
7. **Exposed upland: risk and recovery.** Begin with exposure, soil/rooting, height and thinning records. Some intervention may destabilise the stand; subsequent storm forces safe exclusion zones, specialist contracting, limited salvage, habitat retention and reforestation choices. Assessment: safety first, realistic finances, protected soil and resilient future woodland.
8. **Capstone estate: uncertainty and prioritisation.** Several compartments: commercial mixed-age, old broadleaf, riparian, vulnerable conifer, a disease-affected pocket. Budget means only some can be treated now. Ask the player to produce and defend a ten-year management plan, execute some cycles, revise after surprises, and explain omissions. Success should be a *defensible trade-off profile*, not a single secret optimal prescription.

## Common educational grammar

Across all eight scenarios teach six transferable skills: **(1) diagnose** species, ages/diameters, site, water, past work, hazard and conservation status; **(2) define objectives** for that owner/site; **(3) compare** intervention, protection and non-intervention options; **(4) approve** safe, resourced, lawful work with owner-versus-contractor roles; **(5) observe** actual ecological, economic and structural outcomes over years; **(6) justify and revise** the next cycle. Reduce handholding from Scenario One to Scenario Eight, with contextual tools retained rather than a conventional XP ladder. Distinguish physical forest evidence from modelled scenario projections and unsupported precise probabilities.

## Coverage audit

**Main archetypes sampled:** even-aged productive plantation; established irregular/mixed CCF; neglected mature broadleaf and dieback; initial afforestation/establishment; riparian/wet woods; long-established Atlantic native wood; exposed/storm-damaged plantation; combined multi-stand estate. **Main objectives:** production, timber quality, recruitment, establishment, native conservation, water/soil, resilience, safety, income and planning.

**Not exhaustively taught:** urban/roadside tree risk, coastal shelterbelts, coppice, agroforestry and orchard production, hazel/yew/limestone specialist woodlands, fire response, protected peatland restoration, every invasive/pathogen, public recreational access or detailed licensing. Cover these through optional diagnosis cards or later specialisations rather than falsely promising competence with every forest type.

## Reuse versus additional production scope (NOT a roadmap approval)

- **Reuse from accepted/current Scenario One:** tree/crown and species-aware ecology, individual marking, physical inspection, racks/Work Plan, nursery, browsing, timber/cost, save and Annual Review. Features still under qualification remain **not externally accepted**.
- **Additional moderate features:** new scenario loader/data contract; differing authored initial age/size/species/site distributions; wider species traits and species-specific timber calibration; planting establishment; crop-tree improvement and stand history; site-linked monitoring.
- **Significant new systems:** property-scale budgets/compartment planning (Scenario Eight); hydrology/water impacts (Scenario Five); credible persistent invasive/continuity indicators and controls (Scenario Six); qualified storm activation/windblow/salvage (Scenario Seven); ash disease and safety/reconstitution (Scenario Three).
- **Species priority:** Sitka/Oak/Beech existing; Scots pine, downy birch and common alder have supporting parameter-readiness work, with acknowledged gaps. Rowan/holly/hazel/willow can initially be ecology or minor-canopy roles rather than automatically getting commercial growth models. Ash is a disease/restoration case, not a standard new productive planting crop. Douglas-fir/Norway spruce are optional later commercial depth if needed and empirically supported.
- **Evidence guards:** Do not generalise pure-Sitka density/yield coefficients; do not claim European DEC/DMC numerics are Irish; no universal mixed-species growth bonus; no exact Irish browsing or windthrow probabilities invented; no fixed real-world prices/grant rates in long-term simulation. Regeneration success is sapling recruitment, not seedlings alone.
- **Operational guard:** In high-risk storm/felling cases, player's work is assessment, contractor instruction and safe simulation, not DIY windblow chainsaw handling. Felling/licensing/protected-sites detail must be checked against current official guidance, not invented as a permission-free action.
