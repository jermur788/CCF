## 3. Additional teaching lessons: value, hazards and smallest honest proof

### 3.1 O4 simultaneous nurse and target planting — **recommended candidate for careful design**, not release

- **At year zero:** plant a fast/temporary nurse cohort and slower/intended future crop **at the same time** in authored groups/bands; no mature shelter at planting. This is **one** site-conditional method, alongside pioneer-first then later underplanting, direct mixture and natural regeneration/non-planting. It is not the whole definition of instant new CCF.
- **Learner skill:** evaluate potential shelter vs overtopping competition as two ages/sizes change; decide whether, where and when to remove some nurses while retaining future crop/cover. A good non-plant case should protect valuable existing open habitat rather than automatically reward planting.
- **Real proof required:** scenario/initial land eligibility; separate persisted **role intention** for temporary nurse and target crop (not silently the existing Crop Tree/Fell mark); species/spacing and yearly tree growth; trustworthy positive shelter if claimed, competition, browse and target survival; staged Work Plan nurse removal; after-years 3D and recorded results; old-world/new-scenario identity and protected save tests. **Present growth/crown/juvenile code only demonstrates some competition, not an empirically calibrated positive shelter mechanism.**
- **Evidence:** COFORD Guest & Huss (2012) documents oak+pine/larch/nurse spruce trials and opposite failure risks; Pommerening & Murphy (2004) and European trials are concept/transfer, not Irish parameter tables. Specific webinar wording from owner only. Never invent nurse mix ratios, Irish ideal removal-year or blanket growth multiplier.
- **Scope option:** decide whether O4 core release needs this full causal nurse comparison, or whether it is a separately approved later O4 variation after a simpler plant-vs-natural/no-plant minimum loop.

### 3.2 O3 coppice with standards — **educationally distinctive but at high risk of overloading O3**

- **Unique skill:** distinguish *living coppice stools and vegetative regrowth* from single-tree felling and from normal high-forest thinning. A viable limited hazel coppice rotation may yield smallwood while retaining mature standards; existing ancient habitat, failing stools and severe browsing can make coppicing wrong.
- **Real proof required:** saved stool/multi-shoot identity, prior cut/viability and protected standards, Work Plan coppice cut not generic Fell, annual real stool survival/shoot growth/browse, visual multi-stem regrowth, smallwood cost/yield, multi-cycle observation. Without that, call it explanation-only, not an implemented interactive coppice lesson.
- **Overload:** O3 already proposes neglected Oak/Beech, tree form, ash dieback hazard, owner/contractor safety, veteran retention and selective release. Making *all* of these compulsory in O3's first release risks several unconnected mini-games and weak primary learning. **Recommended review default:** choose one central O3 rehabilitation objective; expose coppice as an optional bounded authored patch *or* defer its full causal feature to O8 estate after single-forest lessons prove effective. Do not create ninth scenario simply for coppicing.
- **Counterexample:** O6 historic Atlantic woodland might need **no coppice**, especially where continuity, epiphytes, old stools/veterans are at risk; never universal 'coppice improves biodiversity'.

### 3.3 Fruit/nut — Stage 1 recognition vs Stage 2 production, not a disguised orchard feature

- **Stage 1 O4:** site-aware forestry recognition/establishment of suitable hazel or nut-bearing species can be a lesson if relevant to planting and woodland suitability. Without new actual yield rules do not show nut sales, orchard pruning, flowering/fruit quantity, harvesting/processing or orchard ROI.
- **Stage 1 O8:** a prospective mixed-use planting opportunity may affect future strategic choice only to the extent existing forest work/budget state honestly supports it; do not silently introduce food production economics via an estate menu.
- **Stage 2, accepted D-054:** first full fruit/nut production slice once Stage 1 forestry is mature. Requires separately accepted crop physiology, market/harvest, tasks and long-run economy. Early planning references do not alter that boundary.
