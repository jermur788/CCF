## 3. Evaluate simultaneous nurse and target crop establishment accurately

The owner's particular observation of Pommerening's *Coppice, Carbon and Catastrophes* describes **fast temporary nurse and slower intended long-term crop planted AT THE SAME TIME on bare land**, not nurse crop planted several years earlier and underplanted later. The nurse becomes a shelter as it grows; **no full shelter exists on day one**; selective nurse removal may be required **before final maturity**. This is ONE possible *instant new CCF* pathway, not its exclusive definition.

Contrast with: (i) pioneer-first then target underplanting, (ii) direct target-species mixture with no temporary nurses, (iii) suitable natural colonisation and **do not plant on valuable open habitat**. Challenge site suitability and decisions with opposite failures: slow nurse never helps vs over-vigorous nurse suppresses oak if release late.

**Evaluate #430 source evidence:** [Guest & Huss, COFORD (2012), *Nursing of oak in Ireland*](https://www.coford.ie/media/coford/content/publications/projectreports/cofordconnects/SM21-LR.PDF); [Pommerening & Murphy (2004), review DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/forestry/77.1.27); continental Czech simultaneous beech/pioneer trials; UK guidance; 2023 webinar notice. COFORD Irish experiences are site-specific, not universal probabilities/spacing/time rules.
