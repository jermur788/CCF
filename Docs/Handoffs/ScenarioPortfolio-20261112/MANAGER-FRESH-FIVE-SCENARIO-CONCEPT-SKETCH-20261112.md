# Fresh concept sketch — five independent CCF forest scenarios

**Created:** 12 November 2026. **Status:** EXPLORATORY PRODUCT DESIGN / NOT A DECISION, SPEC, MILESTONE OR IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORISATION. **Provenance:** new Manager model-derived concept, grounded in inspected current CCF systems and research-index boundaries. No prior Scenario Two–Five curriculum was used as an input or adopted. The current accepted Scenario One and its immutable compatibility requirements are not changed by brainstorming alternatives.

## Design hypothesis

Build five different forest-management questions that share a strong player loop — inspect in 3D; plan work; choose contractor/owner execution; simulate annual time; walk, compare and learn; adapt. Each is an independent property with its own ecological and economic history; player skill, not saves/cash/wood, transfers.

The first five are **forestry-first**; don't introduce broad fruit/nut, survival/crafting or cross-region ecosystems just to create variety if those systems dilute the forestry purpose. This is an initial **Ireland/Atlantic-temperate-biome-oriented** portfolio because the project has its deepest current species/research coverage there; geography and order remain design choices.

## Five scenario proposals

| Proposed scenario | Starting forest and central dilemma | Player's visible repeated decisions and distinctive outcome | Current reuse vs new system debt |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Plantation Inheritance** | Existing planted, even-aged Sitka spruce stand with some Oak/Beech opportunity; timber income competes with developing structure and retaining cover. | Read the stand and plan five-rack access, identify retained crop and seed trees, choose individual felling and protection/planting, scale a demonstrated sample carefully to comparable areas, approve ordinary work, advance years, review and decide again. See an initially uniform stand develop gaps and young cohorts without pretending a single thinning solves CCF. | Strongest reuse: current Scenario One, Rack V2, MFM V1, tree/ecology/annual Work Plan and history, broadleaf species models. Still needs end-to-end demo/review/continued interventions and user test. |
| **2. The Broadleaf Challenge** | Oak-and-Beech mixed stand with variable competition, contrasting shade and recruitment, understorey/browse pressure and uneven opportunity. | Inspect individual broadleaves, retain different crown architectures, make conservative selective openings or leave viable structure, protect/promote natural/planted recruits, return years later and reassess. Avoid equating Sitka sample rules with broadleaf practice. | Reuse Oak/Beech adult and juvenile/browse/understorey and existing general management; new authored broadleaf forest, visual identity and evidence-calibrated species-specific management teaching, broadleaf economics checks. Current MFM auto-treatment is Sitka-specific: do not claim ready generic broadleaf prescription. |
| **3. The Exposed Forest** | Wind-exposed conifer/mixed stand, difficult extraction access and patches of wet/sloping ground; removing trees can increase exposure. | Decide where/when/how much to intervene, plan access, preserve stable groups, respond to actual storm damage if enabled, salvage selectively or retain deadwood, manage profitability and future cover under uncertainty. | Reuse racks, costs, deadwood, mortality, annual time, dormant StormModel 1. Requires *separately approved* storm activation, player-legible risk/exposure/site heterogeneity, credible disturbance presentation and verification; do not assume full soil/hydrology/climate simulation exists. Storm occurrence/calibration is gameplay abstraction, not an Irish forecast. |
| **4. The Recovering Woodland** | Degraded Atlantic woodland with surviving mature trees, thin natural recruitment, competing ground vegetation and browsing; possibly native birch/alder where later supported. | Choose protection, targeted vegetation management, natural regeneration, carefully sited native planting, deadwood/habitat retention and often deliberate non-intervention. Walk recovery/failure as it unfolds. Timber is not the only success measure. | Reuse regeneration, bramble/bracken survival competition, browsing/protection, deadwood/habitat visuals and long-run ecology. Requires restoration objectives/indicators, perhaps richer browsing history, invasive species/habitat/open-ground rules, new species/assets and credible site processes; planting should not be automatically rewarded. High ecology/evidence/design effort. |
| **5. The Living Estate** | One small holding with several separately managed, different-aged and species-composed woodland compartments, shared budget, staff/contractor limits and landscape constraints. | Allocate annual effort among stands, compare treatment histories, retain productive woodland while managing some for habitat and recruitment, adapt to delayed effects or shocks, plan across decades without perfectly balancing every goal. | Reuse the whole forestry loop, per-tree events/history/economy. Needs new multi-compartment property data, scheduling, map/portfolio UI, shared costs/logistics, performance and distinct plot/ecology histories; high cross-system and persistence work. This is the synthesising capstone, not automatically the next cheapest scenario. |

## Suggested player-learning arc

1. **Understand the mechanics** with an inherited plantation and see one full repeated CCF cycle, not just a first cut.
2. **Question a transferred method**: Oak/Beech competition and regeneration demand different judgements, not just different growth coefficients.
3. **Manage uncertainty and unintended consequences**: access, wind and exposure change what is prudent.
4. **Recognise ecological restraint**: recovery, habitat and the option to do no work can be positive choices.
5. **Integrate competing objectives across space and decades**: timber, costs, regeneration and ecological resilience on a property scale.

There is **no prescribed correct tree-by-tree outcome**, fixed interval, absolute profitability ranking or XP ladder. Distinct open-ended credible end-states may count; exact success and fail conditions are **open** and require later product specification.

## Why these ideas suit the real engine — with explicit gaps

Current CCF has a 3D individual-tree/ecology foundation, Sitka/Oak/Beech models of differing evidence precision, canopy/light, regeneration, bramble/bracken survival competition, browsing and basic protection, deadwood, economy, Work Plan, annual historical records, and a Rack V2/MFM first lesson. The D-073 Year Comparison has a **worker-reported** focused Unity Tier-2 PASS, independent T-06 pending; D-074 spacing and D-076 repeat-source packages are **DRAFT**, not running mechanics; the MFM prescription-resolution identity mismatch has static evidence and an authentic verification pending; StormModel 1 is integrated **dormant**, not enabled/default/player-certified. No general multi-scenario loader or multi-stand estate manager is asserted.

This is **capability leverage**, not a release gate assessment for any Scenario 2–5. It does not claim species-specific rainfall/waterlogging, native Atlantic restoration objective, invasive species ecology, actual exposed-site instability, new regional species or multi-compartment property scheduling already exist.

## Risk/effort ordering for concept only

- Scenario 1 is already in development and the strongest **reference implementation**, not yet user-accepted/complete external demo.
- Scenario 2 could be the **lowest-cost genuinely different second forest** if it stays within present broadleaf ecology and requires a newly authored starting stand. It is not free: visual/market/risk/evidence and honest player judgement must pass.
- Scenario 3 is **moderate-to-high cost** because a dormant storm system is not equivalent to credible, interactive environmental risk.
- Scenario 4 is **high cost** because habitat/restoration success, new flora/species and disturbance/browsing mechanisms need new evidence/UX and art.
- Scenario 5 is **highest architectural cost** if it includes multiple simultaneous compartments; it should be scheduled only after forestry systems generalise reliably, with a reduced single-compartment variant if needed.

Relative costs are Manager estimates, **not** calibrated engineering forecasts or commitments.

## Independent-browser study questions

- Is this an effective diversity of questions, or do scenarios 2 and 4 overlap too much?
- Does scenario 3 need a storm-risk model at all, or can exposure be taught via constrained choices and observed stand responses first?
- Is scenario 5 better as a portfolio capstone or does it exceed the first five's reasonable architectural budget?
- What truly different objectives, initial ecology, economic and visual changes, player actions, 20–100-year outcomes and failure modes define each?
- Could a more distinctive fifth forest — e.g. a genuinely uneven-aged highly diverse continuous-cover reference — deliver more forestry depth than a multi-stand property?
- Should an earlier one of the five include restoration/low-intervention management, while storm risk arrives later?
- Which systems are actually integrated/feature-qualified, which are draft/dormant, and what must be built before each scenario can honestly be demoed?
