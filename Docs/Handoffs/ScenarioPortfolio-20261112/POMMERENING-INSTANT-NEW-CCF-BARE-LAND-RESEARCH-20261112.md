## Executive correction

The earlier research summary overemphasised *pioneer establishment first, target-species underplanting later*. That is a separate possible nurse-crop approach; it must **not** replace the owner's described **simultaneous establishment of nurse and target trees**. A third option is a direct multi-species mixed planting without a dedicated temporary shelter role, and a fourth is natural colonisation or non-planting where site/habitat warrants.

“Instant new CCF” (Pommerening's book heading) denotes **designing a future continuous-cover management regime from bare land**, not instant shelterwood conditions or an immediate multi-aged forest. Conventional uniform shelterwood uses mature standing canopy to shelter natural regeneration. A *nurse crop* creates analogous protective effects as the new planting grows; not all nurses are retained as mature final-crop trees, and dominance is a management risk.

### Core causal sequence for **simultaneous nurse and target planting**

1. **Year zero:** Plant suitable site-matched *temporary nurse trees and slower-growing intended main-crop trees simultaneously*, in deliberate spatial rows/bands/groups. There is no pre-existing nurse canopy, so no day-one full shelter.
2. **Establishment:** Nurse species selected for comparatively quick juvenile height/crown development can begin creating side shade, reduced exposure, weed competition relief and altered microclimate. This effect is conditional on achieved growth, suitable spatial separation and site; nurses may fail or grow slower than crop.
3. **Middle stage:** Growing nurse crowns can become competitors, suppressing target crop if overly dense or retained too long. Inspect target height, crown state, leader damage, stem form, light and nurse competition. Prescribe selective phased nurse removal while keeping sufficient useful shelter.
4. **Later stage:** The target crop becomes predominant; future spatial regeneration, structure diversification, continuous selective management and retention depend on actual establishment/survival. Removal of nurse trees is a means to a future stand, not an automatic complete clearfell or proof of immediate unequal-aged structure.

**Crucial teaching twist:** A well-intended nurse planting can **fail in opposite directions**: no shelter when it grows too slowly; or suppression if it grows too quickly and is not removed at the right time. This creates a physically observable, multi-cycle management decision whose consequences are captured by the CCF annual-review loop.

## Genuine alternative management routes for proposed O4 New Farm Woodland

| Route | Timing | Mechanism | Specific player dilemma |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Simultaneous nurse+target (owner-highlighted variation)** | Nurse + long-term crop **planted together at year zero** | Nurses rapidly overtop/create shelter, then are progressively thinned before suppressing main crop | Select site-suitable nurse/crop; where and how mixed; whether to thin nurses now, later or partially, watching shelter-vs-competition |
| **B. Pioneer-first, underplant later** | Establish nurse stand first; introduce sensitive species **only after real shelter exists** | Lower vulnerable establishment exposure, but delayed target age and planting costs | Is later underplanting now appropriate given actual light, access, browse and shelter? |
| **C. Direct long-term mixed crop without temporary dedicated nurse** | Intended main species planted in one or more groups initially | Simple ownership and no planned nurse removal, but exposed/shade-sensitive species may fail and mixtures can compete | Group layout vs intimate mixing, stock survival, later form and thinning |
| **D. Natural colonisation/retain open habitat** | Seed trees/hedge/volunteers arrive organically, or planting not appropriate | Avoid needless disturbance/expense or ecologically harmful afforestation | Is there a real viable seed source and suitable site? Is open habitat valuable and should it remain open? |

These are conditional **game alternatives**, not an accepted formal four-option UI or implication that all approaches work on every soil. They are **one lesson variation inside O4**, not a ninth scenario or a revision to the eight-case order. If one is later adopted, do not auto-credit successful woodland for planting counts; measure target crop survival, form, regeneration and retention of appropriate habitat.

## Engineering and educational proof (no production authorization)

The smallest convincing game comparison could author three similar plantable strips with differing **nurse arrangement** and one explicitly non-plantable habitat pocket. At year zero, mark trees with **purpose** (temporary nurse vs intended retained crop) as a future *scenario-specific plan tag* distinct from accepted current Crop Tree/Fell marks; this tag is **proposed state requiring specification, not implemented**.

On subsequent annual simulation:
- Existing growth and crown interactions may already express some overtopping/competition; **true positive shelter** against wind/frost, damage and ground vegetation must be modelled or honestly labelled **unsupported**, never added as scenic “shelter bonus”.
- Nurses *can* improve protection but may also worsen light competition, and removal can increase exposure. Save tree identity and removal/work/history so lessons survive load; no instantaneous canopy at planting.
- Time/cost and labor for staged nurse management belong in Work Plan only after plan approval; no unmarked automatic thinning. Historical records must say what was actually done, not infer intent from resulting tree patterns.
- At selected **illustrative checkpoints** (not biological constants) inspect Y1 planting/weed/browse, Y5 nurse-target relative heights, Y10–20 need for release, Y30+ altered stand structure; adjust to local dynamics and species. Show risk that Scots pine fails as nurse on unsuitable heavy soil and risk that larch suppresses oak without timely removal.
- A hard evidence gate before shipping: document site-specific main/nurse trait choices, verify a genuine *measured difference in authoritative tree/cell states* across at least two alternative work histories, show rendered differences and review provenance, and confirm save/old-reference compatibility. No blanket +20% nurse growth, unverified frost risk or exact Irish first-thin year.

**Existing production reuse:** exact planted juveniles, browsing and local competition, light/crown competition, Work Plan, timber dispositions, Annual Review, three-species model. **New/proposed:** bare-land/site identity, initial planting-design roles/spatial groups, explicit temporary nurse management and any positive shelter effect model, realistic species palette at supported evidence depths, scenario-specific goals, save routing.
