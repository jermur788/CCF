## Executive decision (under 250 words)

**COPY ONLY, as a later product-owner choice; REUSE CURRENT RULE for the present demo.** There is credible Irish and UK support for a forester deliberately retaining a tree that remains a competitor. Its future timber, seed, habitat, local microclimate, species or stand-structure role can warrant retention, and light crown release is not synonymous with removing every close neighbour. Irish Teagasc explains a real two-way wind dilemma: newly exposed trees can lose mutual shelter and face higher immediate wind vulnerability, while prolonged crowding/delayed thinning can leave trees slender and poorly adapted. A small Irish pulling experiment found favourable tree-form differences after thinning but **did not establish a significant change in critical overturning moment**.

For CCF the separation is essential: **the existing local competition/light model can show some growth and shade consequences, but new Scenario One uses storm model 0 and therefore does not produce wind-damage consequences** from either choice. Tree-specific habitat value, physical wind shelter, mast/seed benefits, future timber and prudential intentions cannot be asserted unless backed by existing on-screen authoritative state. Non-numeric explanatory language is defensible; numerical wind odds, a new objective exception, an accepted retained-neighbour reason field, or active wind mechanics are not.

The present Lesson 3 requirement (at least 30% planned Hegyi Share reduction per confirmed Crop Tree and 40% basal-area safeguard) remains **gameplay calibration**, not Irish forestry doctrine. Different defensible individual removals may satisfy a treatment intention; no professional exact tree-ID answer emerges from the literature. Any player copy would be a **separate, explicitly approved post-demo product change**. Nothing in this report authorises shipping it.

## 1. Questions answered, evidence classes and boundary

**Evidence classes used here:** DIRECT IRISH = study/official guidance about Irish conditions (with empirical vs guidance identified); TRANSFER = UK/continental experiment or guidance, not an Irish coefficient; MODEL DERIVATION = inference about CCF's existing observed state, not measured ecology; GAMEPLAY CALIBRATION = accepted game threshold, not empirical target; UNSUPPORTED = causal outcome or value not evidenced by the source and implemented model. Confidence describes suitability for *qualitative explanation*, not confidence in a numerical predictor.

The research question concerns a dense, roughly 20-year Irish Sitka-spruce plantation transitioning toward mixed Sitka/Oak/Beech. P1's British neighbourhood/frame-tree material and P6's European silvicultural taxonomy are relevant analogues, not measurements of this invented Scenario One stand. Irish Teagasc's transformation advice and published/professional forestry guidance give closer practice context. The DAFM Woodland Improvement Scheme 2023–2027 page was updated 30 March 2026; it confirms that selective improvement, marking, crop-tree release, regeneration, biodiversity and transformation plans are supported activities, **not** the game's unique workflow or objective formula [R1–R5].

**Protected CCF facts at this handoff (from current project handoff/spec/Manager disposition, not new code verification):** confirmed player Crop Trees; MFM sample-to-comparable-area proposals are **not Fell marks**; the Work Plan is the commitment gate; manual player choices take precedence. Lesson 3 teaches a **30% planned Hegyi Share reduction** subject to the **40% basal-area safeguard**. These are accepted gameplay calibration. New Scenario One has **storm model 0**. Do not silently activate dormant StormModel1 or imply a wind outcome in the default game. No lesson, save, model or pre-demo interface changes are authorised [C1–C3].

### Evidence strength by question

The strongest Irish evidence is the **existence** of positive-selection thinning, objectives beyond immediate growth and context-dependent wind risk. The Irish historical windthrow study [R12] is stand-scale; the Irish UCD pulling study [R11] is too small/inconclusive for a causal neighbour release-risk score. The British marteloscope observations [R6–R7] measure disagreement in decisions, not the true quality of the eventual stand. The UK harvesting example [R13] is a genuine operational qualification to blanket delegation, not a claim about all operators.

When a tree already bears authoritative habitat/seed status, copy may describe that status; without it, use cautious intention wording. The existing ability simply to leave a neighbour unmarked already supports the choice without any new UI.

## 4. Removal benefits and the non-monotone wind problem

**Removal benefit.** Teagasc's Irish TranSSFor thinning-regime description [R2] explicitly explains selecting Q-trees and removing neighbours that compete with their crowns, allowing expansion and variable spacing. P1 [R6, §1.1, §1.4] describes varying numbers of competitors removed around different frame trees, not uniformly clearing every tree. This makes release a measurable *competition* teaching example, not an assertion of universally correct inventory.

**Short-term release cost.** Teagasc [R5, 24 Apr 2026] explains loss of mutual support and new exposure when canopy is opened, especially following heavy or late thinning. The historical Irish windthrow model [R12] reports elevated stand-level windthrow incidence after thinning across studied soils. A physical and site-conditional possibility is credible; **the default simulation does not calculate it**.

**Longer-term non-treatment cost.** The Teagasc WINDRISK account [R10] identifies drawn-up stems with narrow crowns and poorly developed roots in dense crops, and potential benefits of timely early thinning. Forest Research [R9] similarly distinguishes early acclimatisation from risky late intervention. P1 warns that unthinned patches can sometimes worsen disturbance effects at susceptible upland sites [R6, PDF p.3]. Thus “leaving the stand alone eliminates wind risk” is **also incorrect**.

**Irish experiment limit.** The González Fernández 2017 field experiment [R11, §6] measured critical turning moments in sampled Sitka trees on wet mineral soil. It reported trends favouring timely thinning but not a statistically significant between-treatment difference in that endpoint, while certain crown/stem-form variables differed. Single-tree pulling with truncated stems also omits canopy-force transfer among neighbours. This should temper generalisations from descriptive research programmes or from visual evidence of thicker stems.

**Spatial/site conditioning.** Wind climate, height, rooting/soil, drainage, topography, treatment timing and prior acclimatisation interact [R5, R10–R12]. Neither wind risk nor crown release should be represented as a single monotonically increasing or decreasing function of “competitors removed.” Teagasc's schematic annual storm percentages in [R5] are not input calibration for this game's site, and the Irish stand-level model is not a valid individual-tree simulator.

## 5. Is a single 'correct' competitor supported? Does P1 contradict UK machine-cab evidence?

**No single mandated competitor.** P1 [R6] investigated selection of frame trees and then competitors in 26 British training marteloscopes, finding that competitor marking attracted much lower agreement than identifying frame trees. P2 [R7] is a separate study of low versus crown thinning judgements; do not switch the attribution, import its agreement categories as quality ratings, or infer that any divergent marking choice is correct. Tree selection is evaluated against local objectives, future development, safety and consequences, not inter-rater consensus.

**Qualified delegation, not scientific conflict.** P1 [R6, PDF p.11/combined p.13, §4–5] says trained machine operators *may* select competitors after a manager identifies frame trees. Forest Research's UK 2006 case studies [R13, executive item 14, Fernworthy p.17] document an **actual exception**: in dense transformation Sitka, crown visibility from the machine cab was inadequate to make a reliable crown-thinning choice; other sites used marked demonstration areas and feller-select successfully. This is different emphasis at different operational scales, **not** a contradiction about crown biology. The game's manager/player demonstration, transparent proposals, review and Work Plan commitment are consistent with recognising that limitation. It does **not** establish a new game safety defect, contractor liability simulation or a right to bypass user confirmation.

## 6. Candidate educational copy — DRAFT ONLY, NO SHIP AUTHORITY

**A — Recommended short general help (3 sentences).**

> Competition Share is a size-and-distance-based measure of pressure from nearby trees, not a verdict that one particular neighbour must be felled. Keeping a neighbour can serve other aims, while thinning and leaving dense areas both involve real-world stability trade-offs. The default Scenario One does not simulate wind damage, and this lesson uses a defined crown-release target rather than a universal forestry rule.

**B — Minimal near-Lesson-3 explanation (2 sentences).**

> There may be good reasons to keep a nearby competing tree; selecting a Crop Tree does not automatically mean felling all its neighbours. This exercise measures a specific crown-release target, and wind damage is not simulated in the default game.

**C — Longer optional help (3 sentences).**

> Removing a competitor may give the Crop Tree growing space, while retaining it may preserve shade, possible habitat, a future timber tree or another management option. Sudden exposure can raise short-term wind vulnerability, but continued dense competition can also weaken long-term stand development; those wind outcomes are not simulated in default Scenario One. Consider real site and tree conditions when interpreting the lesson rather than treating Competition Share as a complete professional judgement.

**D — Most conservative / lowest-claim option (2 sentences).**

> This lesson teaches a game-calibrated local competition target, not the only acceptable way to manage a forest. The default Scenario One does not simulate wind damage.

**Recommendation:** If a future product owner approves copy without new code, A or D is defensible. **No present-tense player-facing UI modification is authorised by this research.** A/B/C remain explicit conceptual drafts; do not imply that the current game measures actual shelter/habitat improvements, and check the exact UI terminology against production code before shipment.

## 7. What must *not* be imported into gameplay or cited as Irish prescription

1. P1's illustrative growing-space, frame-tree-count, H/D or dominance rules as fixed Irish thresholds for the game's Sitka/Oak/Beech mix; they are contextual theoretical prescriptions, **not** a tested local wind-release rule [R6].
2. The accepted **30% planned Hegyi Share reduction / 40% basal-area safeguard** labelled as an Irish statutory, Teagasc or Pro Silva rule; they are **GAMEPLAY CALIBRATION** [C2].
3. Any number from the 2009 historical Irish **whole-stand** windthrow model [R12] as the probability a specific neighbour will fail in an irregular small mixed stand, or as evidence to switch StormModel1 on.
4. Any measured wind benefit from the non-significant UCD small-sample critical-turning-moment results [R11].
5. Teagasc's broad illustrative annual storm/damage percentages [R5] as a fixed local event rate. Geographic exposure and treatment history are unresolved; no such gameplay parameter is approved.
6. British agreement statistics / Fleiss' kappa as a player expertise or marking-quality coefficient; P2's Stoyan-based interpretation categories must not be mislabeled as Landis–Koch [R7].
7. “Habitat improved,” “wind shelter gained,” “seed source protected” or “later income guaranteed” solely because a retained-neighbour label was selected.
8. UK feller-select documentation as proof all operators can/cannot assess crowns, or as a direct mandate to reproduce one operator-selection process in Ireland [R13].

## 8. Evidence needed *before* an actual feature can be proposed

**B. **C. Habitat/retention rationale feature — new SPEC REQUIRED.** Inventory exactly which ecological and economic facts are authoritative and already inspectable (actual species, age, crown/light, defects, recorded habitat trees/deadwood, seed status, economic potential, history). Design only explicit **player intention**, separate from measured result and Work Plan commitment; if intentions persist, specify versioning and read-only historical truth. Test that keeping a competing tree does not suppress its modelled competition, satisfy Share reduction falsely, produce automatic biodiversity credit or become a hidden auto-proposal exception.

Educational copy-only change — approval and proportionate check.** Product owner must decide if/where explanatory caveat belongs; validate wording against live UI, preserve lesson and MFM thresholds, confirm 1280×720/1920×1080 legibility only where relevant, and use a small source/provenance audit rather than running a full Unity qualification for a research report.
