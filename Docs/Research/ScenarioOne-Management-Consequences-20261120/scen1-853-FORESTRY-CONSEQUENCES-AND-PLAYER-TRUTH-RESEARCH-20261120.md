## 1. Executive verdict

**VERDICT — CAUSAL TEACHING PREMISE CREDIBLE; QUANTITATIVE AND PLAYER-VISIBLE VALIDATION INCOMPLETE.** Selective release plausibly transfers growing space to retained Sitka, while retaining canopy preserves shelter and some habitat but can limit broadleaf recruitment. Planting/protecting sessile Oak and European Beech can introduce viable future trees without assuming off-site donors; browsing, light, understorey and species-specific recruitment must remain independent obstacles. Keeping or selling fallen timber and paying for small operations create recognisable ecological/economic choices. These *directions* are supported by Irish experience, forestry guidance and inspectable source pathways; neither ecological coefficients nor the combined Compact30 GUI lesson are field-validated.

The strongest native evidence, **historical #490**, compared only **two** deterministic finite 30 m research arms to elapsed Year 40 (initial **189 Sitka**, **one** Oak and **one** Beech planted at Year 2, common earlier corridor felling, optional Year-5 two-Sitka release). It reported that release increased two selected crop stems' Year-40 DBH by **2.69 and 1.34 cm** and cost **€2,463.08 more net**; Oak and Beech were almost unaffected. Natural cohorts remained Sitka-only, no natural Oak/Beech cohort appeared, and the planted broadleaves remained small-diameter / narrow-crowned relative to their height. These are **model measurements, not field results, exact Compact30 results, 2+2 planting results, or three-cycle adaptive proof**. [R4]

**Top decision-relevant risks:** (1) an overly strong teaching claim that release or planting necessarily produces self-recruiting, resilient continuous cover within 20–40 years; (2) player reporting that conflates model-visible mortality/deadwood, work-related retained deadwood, persistent snapshots and session-only browse observations; (3) tiny-plot monetary examples being understood as a commercially deployable 0.09 ha harvesting operation. A fourth **conditional** risk is uncalibrated wind/stability interpretation after exposing tall Sitka.

**No immediate research-proven source defect or new mandatory science gate has been demonstrated that should stop the owner's exact Compact30 Editor playtest.** Preserve existing integration/source/signature/Unity and owner approval gates, but do not add a fresh research prerequisite. A discrepancy on the *actual candidate* becomes actionable only after it is observed and attributed. ## 3. Existing model credibility — where cause, implementation and observation differ

1. **Canopy and tree release — supported mechanism, approximate law.** Current main computes each cell's canopy from positions, crown radius and height, multiplying fractional gaps; more open space can raise cell light. Adult DBH responds to local competition while height largely follows age/site; density mortality follows a separate source route. Thus removing *which* Sitka matters, not merely harvest percentage. This matches selective thinning/crop-tree silviculture [G1, G2] but the lateral shading law, growth intercepts, Hegyi coefficients and compact edge effects are **game abstractions**, not calibrated hemispherical light or individual-tree Irish forecasts. [S1][S2]

2. **Juvenile recruitment — appropriately multi-bottleneck in structure.** The annual ecology step updates competition, canopy, adult growth and self-thinning, then cohort growth, seed rain, establishment and promotion. Seed arrives by species and local donor; cohort survival and height growth consider light, browse and bramble/bracken pressure, and the management layer grows planted individuals and understorey. These are meaningful directions [A/B/C], but annual coefficients and order-of-update effects require sensitivity checking. A seedling count is **not** a proven durable sapling stand; external broadleaf seeds must not be presumed. Irish oak work shows heavy canopy and competition may prevent recruitment, and Irish Sitka transformation trials recorded no natural regeneration after repeated treatments. [S1–S3, E1, E3, E4]

3. **Browsing/protection — real ecology, simplified state.** Model treats browse pressure as a 0–1 *impact driver*, not measured deer density, and has species/height vulnerabilities and exact-position shelter access; plant and naturally arising juveniles use shared principles. Irish exclosures demonstrate the effect of grazing on woodland regeneration, but also vegetation shifts under exclusion. British fallow-deer seasonal preferences are transfer evidence, **not** Irish species probabilities. The scenario default 0.2, shelter durations and exact response/penalty are **GAMEPLAY CALIBRATION**. Annual Review explicitly labels planted-juvenile browse history as *session-only*, and says so after load; it should not imply persistent long-term browse injury data that are not saved. [S1, S3, S5, E4–E6]

4. **Understorey — key counterweight to 'more light is better'.** Independent bramble and bracken covers in model 2 contribute a juvenile-survival penalty; selective removal is an actual work-order path. The botanical mechanism and mixed concealment/competition story are plausible, but **bramble browse refuge, graminoid causality and persistent form damage are not established production effects** by the current source/evidence. #490 exported understorey but did **not analyse** its trajectories. Do not assert that its tiny release harmed or helped understorey. [S2, R4, E3–E6]

5. **Density/self-thinning — mechanistically present, numerical extrapolation open.** At main, adult density-mortality can remove living Sitka, and accepted D-061 makes mixed-species occupancy **ordinal species-aware** while preserving the 100%-Sitka compatibility case. The actual historic two-arm #490 declines and narrow Oak crown are useful stress signals, **not independent evidence that real Irish 0.09 ha mixed woods should lose that number of stems or produce that crown shape**. Broad & Lynch 2006a fitted Irish *even-aged stand* B/N/H growth; 2006b tested source-data representativeness, not an untouched dynamic prediction of mixed irregular CCF. Pretzsch/del Río species equivalence is European transfer architecture, not an Irish oak/beech density coefficient. [S1, R4, E7–E9]

6. **Wind and habitat — distinguish potential from events.** Main exposes a **diagnostic** wind-risk score based on slenderness, light and recent openings with explicitly game-tuned severity bands, distinct from a storm death probability. #490 had StormModel0, so its survival/mortality record **cannot** judge released trees' storm survival. Irish/UK guidance confirms wind risk changes with exposure, rooting, height, soil and thinning history, including cases where deferral/no-thin is prudent. Fallen deadwood has habitat value, but tree-by-tree biodiversity or species richness must not be inferred from a simple log count or a 3D prop. [S1, S4, E1, G1, G4]

7. **Money/Work Plan — correct narrative shape, not real tariff.** Manager settles only approved work, then ecological year; grouped harvest is one job, while owner versus contractor affects resources/cost. WorkPlanView shows sale/keep/deadwood dispositions and an explicitly named *small-job minimum*. The **€2,500** example is project game calibration and cannot be presented as a real nationwide contractor minimum. Teagasc notes marginal first-thinning returns, sawlog-quality benefit later and economies of scale. Game timber prices/costs are pedagogical scenario quotes with no validated Irish 0.09 ha mobilisation curve, access safety or owner labour pricing. [S3, S6, E10, G2, G3]

## 5. Counterfactual management choices — plausible, not simulated comparisons

**A. No work after initial arrival.** Advantage: preserves cash, immediate canopy/shelter and may avoid extra fresh thinning exposure on vulnerable sites. Countervailing risks: crowded crop trees, loss through density mortality, little light under existing Sitka, and no imported Oak/Beech seed. **INFERENCE**: a rational choice for a near-term budget or high-wind concern, but not a reliable means of achieving a mixed-origin broadleaf forest. #490's quiet **post-initial-access-and-planting** decades are *not* a completely untouched forest treatment.

**B. Limited deliberate release + 2+2 planting/protection + reassessment.** Potential gain: selected retained crop Sitka can grow better, and stock purchase can overcome missing local broadleaf donors; repeat observations permit adjustment. Trade-off: up-front planting/shelter spending, possible browsing/vegetation competition, small harvest negative net, lost local cover. **Partial support:** #490 shows one kind of release, **not** repeated 2+2 planting or finished Compact30. An ecologically meaningful decision could be to postpone more removal if a promising sheltered juvenile is doing better under cover.

**C. Aggressive felling/oversized gap.** Conditional gain: light and some early-established regeneration may increase. Conditional loss: crop capital, wind-firmness during vulnerable periods, deadwood/canopy retention goals, bracken/bramble proliferation, labour and mobilisations. Neither 'more open' nor 'more planting' universally wins. No aggressive-run data in #490; presenting a numerical advantage or a fixed threshold would be **invented**.

**D. Same-species shelter choice.** Shelter is a paid risk-reduction tool, especially before the tree grows above browse reach; it is **not** always economically or ecologically essential if browsing pressure is low, planting site unsuitable or landowner labour scarce. Comparing #490 sheltered Oak against unsheltered Beech confounds species and planting position; no valid treatment effect can be estimated. The first game objective of 2 Oak/2 Beech makes the conceptual choice legible, but only a controlled native comparison could quantify it.

**E. Leave deadwood vs extract a small lot.** Keeping fallen stems avoids additional work, preserves decomposing habitat and leaves cash unchanged; sale might recover some value but can fail to cover a contractor visit. Source evidence supports the choice and accounting. Whether a given outcome is best depends on safe access, decay, assortment, merchantability, intervention timing and owner goals. The current monetary model does not turn one tree's revenue into an Irish commercial forecast.

These are **directional counterfactuals** grounded in mechanisms and practice. No extra model arms were fabricated.

## 6. Small-stand and economic interpretation

- **Exactly 900 m²**, not a representative plantation compartment or a nationally scalable commercial sample. Even if a roster has many stems, per-hectare conversion does **not** make one tiny visit, extraction, edge exposure, roads, contractor travel, work safety or grant eligibility behave as a 1-ha stand. The 175-stem post-H1 arithmetic and four G1 positions remained **unsigned/conditional** at the consulted milestone. H1 pre-existing removals are **not** player timber revenue, ecological annual felling or management success. [P2]
- **Finite open-land boundary is legitimate.** #484 measured significant *hypothetical* missing-neighbour competition contributions for some near-edge focal trees **only under an assumed off-plot stand**; later owner clarification chose an authored bounded woodland beside open land, not a hidden neighbouring forest. Therefore do **not** run another exterior-tree/buffer study, assume hedge donors or invent off-site Oak/Beech seed. A small plot has more edge influence and owner-defined geometry than broad yield tables permit. [R3]
- **Commercial scale:** Teagasc says first thinning can be marginal/break-even and that higher-quality assortments and economies of scale matter in later cuts. This justifies teaching a negative-cash selective release, but **not** deriving the game €2,500 minimum from real Irish minimum-call-out observations. Grouping work into one simulated visit is sensible pedagogy; isolated 0.09-ha economics should carry a 'teaching scenario/illustrative quote' label. Formal grant terms or real sale prices are **not** assumed. [E10, G2, G3]
- **Time and age:** a Year-40 game tick is 40 simulated years from scenario start, not necessarily chronological tree age 40. #490 trees were initially age 20 and planted 'nursery' juveniles had nonzero age at planting. Its Year-40 Oak/Beech were biologically approximately 42 years old; **Mature** is a game interaction/art stage, not proof of biological maturity, reproductive stand or old-growth habitat. Persistent crown narrowness deserves a visual-truth check rather than immediate coefficient replacement. [R4]
- **Biodiversity/stability:** deadwood volume, number of species planted and cover heterogeneity are *indicators/proxies*, not field-measured wildlife, long-term ecological continuity or quantified resilience. Even a favourable broadleaf visual should not become an unsupported biodiversity score. [E4, E8]

## 7. Specific player-language hazards / distinctions

1. **'CCF established' or 'resilient mixed forest achieved' by a fixed early year** — unsupported if based merely on 2+2 planted individuals, mean canopy, or a few recruited Sitka. Prefer an explicitly provisional **management progress** interpretation and preserve the accepted future M-A completion-policy gate; research does not revise it.
2. **'More light = successful natural Oak/Beech regeneration'** — wrong without local seed, suitable seedbed, early survival, browsing escape and sapling promotion. #490 found no naturally recruited Oak/Beech. The current Annual Review already correctly states an opening is an opportunity, **not guaranteed regeneration** [S5:L597]; preserve that principle.
3. **'This many deer / this percent guaranteed browse'** — project pressure 0–1 and game-tuned damage are not Irish deer density or empirical probability. **Current UI correctly tags session-only browse events** and missing post-load records; check it survives combined Compact UI.
4. **'Deadwood new this year' as all causes.** In main, normal manager OnEnable subscribes to mortality; a death triggers a persistent log and visual [S3:L510–555]. #490 harness reportedly disabled manager, so its zero deadwood records are an **artefact-consistent measurement**, not a verified normal-game bug [R5]. AnnualReviewView's 'Retained deadwood (new this year)' currently reads **report.deadwoodCreatedM3**, while 'Fallen deadwood on site' reads the snapshot. The annual work report explicitly increments harvest/retention and recorded storm material, but the biological death callback directly adds a log record; its death volume is **not clearly counted in that annual-report field**. This may be **intentional narrower semantics**. Verify on a normal-enabled year, and distinguish *new retained from work* from *all new biological deadwood* rather than immediately editing code. [S3:L1249–1374, S5:L600–609]
5. **'Wind risk high = windthrow X%' or 'thinning = safety'** — a gamified diagnostic label is not a forecast or wind climate measurement [S1:L1795–1849, G4]. #490 storms were off.
6. **'€2,500 is what an Irish contractor will quote for these exact two stems'** — this is game calibration. The Work Plan already calls out a small-job minimum [S6:L289–346]; preserve grouping and cost causality while avoiding real-tariff wording.
7. **Rack language in Compact30.** Main WorkPlanView still has historic 'Extraction racks' section [S6:L289–300] for the Enlarged80 workflow; it is **not evidence the final Compact player sees this**. Validate the actual composed branch's filtering/routing rather than importing a historic UI screenshot or discarding a working view wholesale.
8. **'Mature Oak/Beech' from art or stage.** #490 showed a young/narrow Oak crown while age and height grew, and 'Mature' was a shared game stage. Any player-facing label must distinguish model life stage from tree size/seed maturity and appearance.

## 9. Findings rejected / scope exclusions

- Reject **natural Oak/Beech recruitment guaranteed by Year 40** without mature donors, appropriate seed rain and successful juveniles. Also reject 'planting always improves biodiversity'; unsuitable planting could compete with open-habitat values.
- Reject **thinning always improves all species** and **no work means no forest change**. Stand-level averages can mask local release and subsequent canopy reclosure.
- Reject extrapolating #490's **two arms, one Oak + one Beech, storm-off research** to multiple adaptive player cycles, the **new 2+2 Compact world**, commercial yield prediction, a player-tested 3D interface or real Irish wind losses.
- Reject the outdated claim **zero #490 deadwood proves live game's deadwood code is broken**. Correct lineage in #490 issue erratum [R5]; verify only under an enabled normal manager if material.
- Reject treating Broad & Lynch 2006b's panel audit as independent mixed CCF dynamic predictive validation, or inserting Europe-derived broadleaf density/mixture-bonus coefficients without Irish validation.
- Reject speculative new donor hedgerows, exterior forest buffers, grass/animal agents, complex rhododendron ecology, novel finance/grant schemes, wind model replacement, or new cross-system save features for the initial playtest. No alternative product/spec approval can be inferred from this report.
- Do not displace #820/#830 gameplay, #836 visuals, #838 floor, #844 Manager, #425 overall ownership, or unrelated Multi #730. Their separate source and runtime evidence cannot be silently combined into one qualified build.
