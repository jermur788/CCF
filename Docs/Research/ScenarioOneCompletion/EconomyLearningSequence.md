# Economy learning sequence (Workstream P)

**Status:** teaching proposal. Copy and sequencing only; **no economy values change**. Wording treats every [C]/[D] value as a *scenario rule*, never an Irish rate.

## 1. Learning outcomes (economic)

By Year 25 a beginner should be able to say:

1. "A contractor visit has a minimum cost. On a small forest like this, thinning costs more than the timber is worth at first."
2. "Bigger, better trees are worth much more per tonne (sawlog vs pulp). Keeping good trees growing is the economic point of an early thinning."
3. "What I leave standing is capital. Cutting more earns more now and leaves less to grow."
4. "Planting, shelters, pruning and clearance cost money now. Some benefits are uncertain or only appear much later."
5. "My own time is limited, and I can't safely fell commercial timber myself."

## 2. Sequence (tied to the stage arc in `ObjectiveRedesign.md`)

| When | Moment | Teaching line (draft) | Mechanism shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start (S0) | HUD help | "You start with €12,000. In this scenario, timber sales are your only income." | Cash |
| First Tree Inspection of a Crop Tree (S3) | "If felled now" estimate | "Small stems sell mainly as pulp. Larger, straighter stems can become sawlogs, worth about two and a half times as much per tonne in this scenario." | Assortments |
| First Work Plan with a thinning (S4/S5) | Thinning card | "In this scenario a contractor visit costs at least €2,500. This forest is very small (0.16 ha), so an early thinning will cost more than its timber earns. You are investing in the trees you keep." | Minimum, scale |
| Same | "What you are leaving" (P3) | "Standing trees left: worth about €1,450 if sold today — still growing." | Capital |
| Before an unaffordable approval / purchase | Warning | "After this you would have €1,900 — less than one harvest visit (€2,500). Timber sales are your only income." | Cash trap prevention |
| First Annual Review MONEY (S6) | MONEY section | "Small-job minimum −€2,403: the visit cost more than its timber was worth." | Minimum, explained after experience |
| First planting (S8) | Planting card | "Saplings, planting time and shelters are paid now. Survival is not guaranteed." | Establishment cost |
| First pruning (S11) | Pruning card | "Pruning can produce knot-free timber in decades. This scenario pays no extra for it — you are choosing quality for the future." | No premium |
| First Leave-as-deadwood choice | Felling outcome | "Leaving these stems as deadwood gives up about €15 of timber sales; the felling work is still paid." | Opportunity cost |
| Second cycle (S12) | Thinning card | "The trees you kept are larger now. Compare this visit's timber value with your first." | Value growth |
| After storms | Salvage | "Salvage costs more than normal harvesting, and value falls as stems decay. Leaving them is also a valid choice." | Salvage trade-off |

## 3. What not to teach

- That CCF is intrinsically more or less profitable than clear-fell ([ECON]: conditional on regeneration success and structure complexity).
- Any figure as a national rate: the €2,500 minimum, €45/h, sapling and shelter prices are scenario values.
- That pruning pays.
- Grants (not modelled).

## 4. Measuring it (playtest)

Interview questions B5 and B6 in `BeginnerPlaytestProtocol.md`: "Why did the thinning cost you money?" and "What do you get for the trees you didn't cut?" Codes: SCALE-OK / ERROR-BLAME (the player thinks they did it wrong) / NO-IDEA.
