# Species, site and system progression

**Status:** RECOMMENDATION. Covers task Parts 6–12. Evidence tiers come from the **CCF Species Evidence Pack v1** (project file) using its codes: [A] direct Irish empirical, [B] authoritative guidance, [T] transfer, [I] inference, [C] calibration needed, [U] unsupported; QNT / ORD / QUAL / INS resolution. No coefficients are proposed here.

## 1. Rule for when a species enters

A species enters a scenario only when **all** hold:

1. it changes a decision the player makes in that scenario's lesson;
2. its evidence supports the depth at which the scenario uses it (an ordinal role is enough for a pioneer that colonises gaps; a merchantable timber role needs growth and assortment evidence);
3. the systems it needs exist (shade casting, site, density, markets).

Unequal modelling depth is deliberate (Species Evidence Pack: "viable only if species are allowed unequal modelling depth"). Variety alone is never a reason.

| **Western hemlock / western red cedar** | Biologically strong CCF fit [T]; **quantitatively blocked** in Ireland | **Not before S5**; likely post-core (sandbox/later scenario) after Irish quantitative research | Shade-tolerant underplanting; hemlock's free regeneration as a management problem | — |

**Scots pine early:** yes — it is the best early addition because one species carries two lessons: light-demand contrast with Sitka (productive forestry) and a native-woodland role (restoration contexts), and it has GROWFOR-level growth evidence.

**Douglas fir in S3 as a planting option, not an existing crop:** this exploits its strongest teaching value (site matching) without needing its markets, thinning response or irregular-stand behaviour until S5. If GROWFOR extraction is not complete when S3 is built, S3 can ship with Scots pine, birch and alder and add Douglas fir later without changing the lesson.

## 3. Site progression (Part 7)

The manager's suggested sequence is **endorsed**:

| Scenario | Site |
|---|---|
| S1 | Fixed, uniform (Class III; neutral site map if the site system later exists — expansion proposal "neutral Scenario One map" keeps anchors bit-identical) |
| S2 | Still uniform. The lesson is *temporal* (history, reassessment); adding space would confound it |
| S3 | **Meaningful variation begins**: three ordinal axes — moisture/drainage, fertility, exposure — as patches within the holding. Species-site suitability shown as ordinal facts with evidence labels |
| S4 | Site present; matters through regeneration (birch on wet, oak failing on waterlogged) |
| S5 | Site present but not the focus |
| S6 | Full variation across compartments; exposure interacts with storms if the storm–site link is accepted |

The Species Evidence Pack warns against a one-dimensional suitability score: keep the three axes separate and show them separately in the map and inspection.

## 4. Economic progression for small landholders (Part 8)

| Stage | Economic idea | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Contractor minimum exists; small thinnings lose money; owner time for planting/protection | **Low** — present as a fact of small woodlands; never a failure route |
| S2 | Grouping work into one contractor visit; timing harvests; owner does marking/pruning; small timber lots | **Low–medium** — first real timing choice (more so if the holding is larger; open decision) |
| S3 | Species-dependent planting/protection costs; investment with distant return | Medium |
| S4 | Protection economics: shelters per tree vs fencing a group; the cost of waiting | Medium |
| S5 | **Strategic**: harvest income must fund future operations; quality affects value; harvest frequency vs minimum charge; liquidity | **High (first strategically demanding scenario)** |
| S6 | All of the above across compartments; finite cash and labour | High, from competing priorities rather than scarcity |

Owner-does-planning/marking while contracting machinery is already the IMPLEMENTED structure (harvest contractor-only; owner time for other work) and matches the economics report (owner labour suits inspection, marking, planting, protection, light pruning). Access constraints (extraction racks, distance to road) are Stage 2 crossover and not needed for the curriculum.

Grants: the economics report says grants are a scenario-policy layer, separate from real costs. Recommend **no grants before S6**, and only then if accepted as a scenario policy, to avoid teaching that forestry decisions follow grant schedules.

Prices: the Species Evidence Pack warns that 2026 Irish timber prices are storm-depressed. Do not calibrate long-run species prices from them; B1 may represent depressed salvage prices only through an explicit, labelled scenario condition (separate decision).

## 5. Timber quality and pruning (Part 9)

| Stage | What the player learns | System |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Crop Trees are "future timber trees" (D-013); pruning work exists for them (D-014) | IMPLEMENTED; no value consequence |
| **S2** | **Pruning as investment**: what you do to a Crop Tree now affects value decades later; a pruned clear stem is visible; no premium is paid now and none is promised | Existing pruning + clear-stem record; honest "no premium configured" (D-044 defers premium). **Optional:** visible form class if quality state v1 is pulled forward (open decision) |
| S3–S4 | Form differences between species (oak form needs management; birch low value) shown as descriptions | Text only unless quality state exists |
| **S5** | **Quality-led selection**: form class, browse-forked leaders, pruned sections → assortment eligibility; biological growth ≠ merchantable value | Per-tree quality state (W4, save bump), species assortments without fabricated prices |
| S6 | Integrated | — |

Rationale for basic pruning in S2: it is the cheapest way to make "Crop Tree" mean something beyond competition, it fits the reassessment theme (the previous owner pruned some trees), and it needs no new system. A full grading system before S5 would be premature: the player must first understand stand management, and the market evidence (T4/T5) does not yet exist.

## 6. Regeneration progression (Part 10)

The proposed sequence is **largely right but mis-timed at the start**, because vegetation competition (Model 2) and low browsing (0.2 + shelters) are already in Scenario 1.

| Stage | Regeneration teaching |
|---|---|
| S1 | Light + natural regeneration (seed present) + planting + vegetation competition + light browse with shelters. Named limiting factor (T1) |
| S2 | Reading regeneration outcomes others caused; **releasing advance regeneration**; when bramble clearance was wasted vs useful |
| S3 | **Seed source and species × site**: birch flush in large wet openings; Scots pine needs large openings; no broadleaf regeneration without broadleaf seed trees |
| S4 | **Diagnosis**: multiple causes in different places; browse shifts composition; gap size by species; protection strategy. Evidence, not verdict (T3) |
| S5 | **Maintaining recruitment continuously** under an irregular canopy; deciding where regeneration is not wanted |
| B2 | Recruitment at landscape browse pressure; accepting slow or absent recruitment |

So the "LATE: diagnose why" stage arrives in S4, not at the very end — it is the prerequisite for irregular management.

## 7. Browsing as a central problem (Part 10–11)

Present from S1 at low pressure (IMPLEMENTED, D-023). **Central in S4**, where pressure is high, species differ in palatability (ordinal, per the pack: Douglas fir, Norway spruce, hemlock, cedar and rowan of higher concern than Sitka), and fencing becomes a real option. The pack is explicit that browse should be split into preference, height suppression, form damage and mortality and that one universal coefficient is not defensible; S4 should use ordinal classes with [C] calibration, and S5 needs browse history for form damage.

## 8. Disturbance progression (Part 11)

| Option | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Experienced in Scenario One | **No.** Storms stay off by default (D-050 dormant; activation policy open). A random storm in the first scenario confounds the core lessons and Storm Model 1 has a neutral site/edge profile |
| Possible but not required in S1 | Only as an explicit player opt-in after completion ("continue with storms on"), if the activation policy allows |
| Introduced deliberately | **Yes — branch B1 After the Storm**, after Scenario 2 |
| Central to a dedicated scenario | Yes (B1) |
| Background risk | From S5 onward, at the frozen Model 1 profile, unannounced |

B1 is cheap (Storm Model 1, salvage through the existing grouped harvest path, an authored disturbed start generated by the storm model itself) and teaches salvage vs retention vs regeneration vs stability. Snapped stems and site-directional exposure remain open D-050 items and are not prerequisites.

## 9. Native woodland / restoration (Part 12)

**Placement: middle-late, as branch B2 after Scenario 4.**

- **Not early:** it needs species strategies, site, browse diagnosis and fencing to avoid becoming "plant natives, fence deer".
- **Not core:** its new systems are the most expensive (open-habitat state, standing deadwood, possibly hazel resprouting) and its key lesson — restraint, ecological objectives — appears in smaller form in S2 (an area needing nothing), S5 (not over-managing a good forest) and S6.
- **Design guards against "native = good":** a valuable open glade that should not be planted; holly that is ecologically normal, not a weed; deer pressure that may make planting futile; deadwood as habitat; a timescale of decades; and the possibility that the best plan is nearly no work. Its completion uses an owner-declared ecological objective, not species counts.
- **Open user decision:** promote to core if the ecological track is a product priority.
