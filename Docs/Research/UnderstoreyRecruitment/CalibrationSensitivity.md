# Calibration and sensitivity

43,200 virtual output rows compare five response forms × three strengths × five processes × three light levels × four covers × two browse levels × protection on/off × seed availability on/off × three clearance schedules × two horizons. Evidence: candidate_sensitivity.csv.gz, candidate_ranges.csv and candidate_summary.json. Reproduce with Tools/Verification/UnderstoreyRecruitment/candidate_sweep.py. These are dimensionless response experiments, not Unity trajectories, physical stems, predicted recruitment, or empirical calibration.

The virtual patch starts with abundance 1. Establishment adds R×L, optionally reduced by the vegetation multiplier. Survival subtracts abundance×e separately from browse loss; growth accumulates light times the growth multiplier and separate browsing response. Browse coefficient 0.04 and growth coefficient 0.9 are C for this experiment; they are not presented as fitted production browsing parameters. Protection eliminates the browse terms only. Cover returns 30% toward its fixed initial target annually (current D placeholder). Clearance sets virtual cover to zero: importantly this toy does NOT remove tree juveniles, whereas production clearance does. Its clearance advantage is therefore an optimistic competition-only contrast, not a treatment recommendation.

With no new arrivals and full cover, survival models compound strongly: even k=0.15 produces roughly 0.85^20≈0.039 surviving abundance under a unit response. k=0.60 produces roughly 0.4^20≈0.000000011. This is a pathology warning for prolonged equal vulnerability, not an estimate of Irish survival. Establishment-only cannot remove existing stock; growth-only does not lower survival directly. A plausible candidate needs a height/stature-limited vulnerability window before any production adoption.

Sensitivity is dominated by process placement, duration of vulnerability, cover recovery, and coefficient strength. Response shape changes low-cover behaviour materially. The light×cover option weakens the penalty in shade but is an additional unvalidated assumption. Tree-light mortality already exists and must not be hidden inside vegetation mortality. Species success and promotion timing cannot be inferred from the virtual outputs: it lacks production age bands, capacity, mast, canopy change and promotion. Species-specific LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH classes were considered but no defensible empirical ordering was found; arbitrary classes would add unsupported precision.

The English oak survival observation checks direction and broad plausibility only. It cannot validate any of these constants independently of other losses. Next calibration must test a shared short-height vulnerability window plus low/central/high competitor survival strength inside a disposable Unity adapter, retain separate loss ledgers, and run seeded mixed-species/protected management worlds before gate reconsideration.
