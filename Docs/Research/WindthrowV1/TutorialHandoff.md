# Tutorial handoff: teaching storms and stability (for a later pedagogy packet)

**Status:** handoff only. Current tutorial files are not edited. Write copy in the style of `Docs/Research/ScenarioOnePedagogy/TutorialCopyBank.md` (pedagogy branch).

## Learning points

1. **Thinning can improve growth but alter stability.** Removing neighbours gives crop trees room; for a few years the trees around a new opening are more exposed to wind.
2. **Risk depends on stand, site and context:** height, slenderness (H/D), how recently neighbours were removed, open edges, soil. None of these alone decides.
3. **Heavy opening is not automatically wrong.** It can be a deliberate trade: faster release and fewer visits, against a few years of higher exposure.
4. **One storm does not prove a treatment was wrong,** and a calm decade does not prove it was right. Judge decisions by their reasons and their range of outcomes.
5. **Storms are disturbance, not just loss:** a storm creates gaps, light, deadwood and salvage choices, and can start regeneration.
6. **Repeated light thinning builds stability over time**, as trees grow stouter crowns and stems. Evidence direction [B]; no "correct percentage".

## Teaching moments (event-driven, not scripted)

| Trigger | Lesson card (≤ 3 sentences) |
|---|---|
| First tree shown as "watch" or "exposed" | What the stability state means and its reason line |
| First thinning that moves trees to "exposed" | "You opened the canopy here. These trees will be more exposed to wind for a few years." |
| First storm | What happened, why trees near openings and tall slender trees failed, what salvage is |
| First salvage decision | Money vs deadwood vs doing nothing; all are legitimate |
| A storm gap shows regeneration (years later) | Disturbance as opportunity |

## What not to teach

- A correct thinning percentage (Teagasc's ≈ 20 % basal-area guidance can be mentioned as *Irish practice guidance*, never as a rule the game enforces).
- A numeric storm chance.
- That unthinned stands are safe.
