Title: What is Continuous-Cover Forestry?

Continuous-cover forestry keeps a forest continuously wooded while it is managed. Instead of clear-felling the whole stand, trees are removed selectively and new generations establish beneath and between those that remain.

Progression:
- BEFORE — Even-aged Sitka plantation
- SELECTIVE TRANSFORMATION — Mature trees retained; openings allow regeneration
- DEVELOPED CCF STRUCTURE — Productive trees, broadleaf diversity and new generations

Principles:
- Keep forest cover
- Manage repeatedly over time
- Encourage regeneration
- Build varied structure

Eyebrow: INTRODUCTION
Title: How CCF works over time

CCF is a cycle of decisions, observation and adaptation. Retain continuous forest cover as you create space for new generations and review how the stand develops.

Five-card copy (preserving five supplied cards, combining review/repeat):
1. Observe the stand
   Read its structure, regeneration and growing conditions.
2. Retain and select
   Keep productive trees and select competing trees for removal.
3. Create space and light
   Remove selected competitors to make suitable conditions for young trees.
4. Regenerate and establish
   Encourage natural regeneration and establish other species, combining productive conifers with broadleaf diversity.
5. Review and repeat
   Observe the response, adapt decisions and repeat management over many years.

Footer: Retained trees, regeneration and varied ages and sizes build a continuously covered forest over time.
